Tutor notes · how this lesson runs (≈45 min)
Zoom setup: you share the screen and click everything; Elina only talks. Tap ⭐ +1 in the top bar each time she says a full English sentence (it's the score for the whole lesson). Each slide shows its target minutes.
Pattern of the day: "I am a ___." (who I am in my costume) + "I have a ___." (a thing I hold or wear) + "What are you? What do you have?"
French-speaker traps: "I am witch" → I am a witch (English needs a); mixing I am and I have ("I have a witch" 😂); "I have ten years" → I am ten; the silent French H: "I 'ave a 'at" → I have a hat; describing words go first: a black hat (not "a hat black").
Culture: Halloween is much bigger in the U.S. than in France: costumes, jack-o'-lanterns, and kids going door to door for candy ("trick-or-treating"). Talk about the tradition itself.
Correcting: never "No." Repeat it back correctly ("Yes! You are a witch and you have a hat!") and let her say it again for the star.

Sound spot · OO3 min

👻 The Ghost Sound: Boo or Book?

Le son du fantôme : Boo ou Book ?
The long OO is the ghost sound: round lips and a long sound, booooo! moon, broom 👻. The short OO is quick and relaxed: look, book, good. Not « louk », but look!Le OO long, c'est le son du fantôme : lèvres rondes et son long, bouuuh ! moon, broom. Le OO court est rapide et détendu : look, book, good. Pas « louk », mais look !

Listen. Which one did you hear? Then say both!Écoute. Lequel tu as entendu ? Puis dis les deux !

Look! A good ghost on a broom says "Boo!" 👻🧹Regarde ! Un gentil fantôme sur un balai dit « Bouh ! »
Tutor prompts
Press Mystery word: it plays one word from a pair; she says which one. Then she says a word and you guess. The purple word in each pair is the long OO. Finish with the twister: slow, normal, fast. Bonus: French speakers often say look and book with a long « ou »; keep them short. Long OO words from today: boo, broom, moon, spooky.
