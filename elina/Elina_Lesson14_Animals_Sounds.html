Tutor notes · how this lesson runs (≈45 min)
Zoom setup: you share the screen and click everything; Elina only talks. Tap ⭐ +1 in the top bar each time she says a full English sentence (it's the score for the whole lesson). Each slide shows its target minutes.
Pattern of the day: "A ___ says ___. It lives ___." + "It eats ___." The key is the -s: it says, it lives, it eats.
French-speaker traps: "It live on a farm" → It lives (he/she/it = -s); "A cow make moo" → A cow says moo (French "fait"); "She lives" for a cow → It lives (animals = it); "a owl" → an owl, an elephant; a silent final -s ("it live") → make the -s heard (Sound spot). Last lesson was "people eat" (no -s); today is "it eats" (-s).
Fun fact for her: animals "speak" differently in each language: cocorico → cock-a-doodle-doo, ouaf → woof, coin-coin → quack.
Correcting: never "No." Repeat it back correctly ("Yes! It lives on a farm!") and let her say it again for the star.

Sound spot · final -S2 min

🐍 Don't Forget the -S!

N'oublie pas le -S !
With it, the verb ends with -s, and you can hear it! Sometimes it's a snake: eats, sleeps 🐍 sss. Sometimes it's a bee: lives, says 🐝 zzz.Avec « it », le verbe finit par -s, et on l'entend ! Parfois, c'est un serpent : eats, sleeps (sss). Parfois, c'est une abeille : lives, says (zzz). En français, le -s final est souvent muet. En anglais, on l'entend !

Listen. Which one did you hear: with -s or without? Then say both!Écoute. Lequel tu as entendu : avec le -s ou sans ? Puis dis les deux !

A snake eats, sleeps, and says hiss! 🐍Un serpent mange, dort et fait « hiss » !
Tutor prompts
This is today's grammar out loud: it says, it lives, it eats. The purple word in each pair has the -s. Note: says sounds like "sez". Press Mystery word: it plays one word; she says which one (with or without -s). Then she says one and you guess: if she drops the -s, "guess" the wrong one so she hears why it matters. Finish with the twister: slow, normal, fast.
