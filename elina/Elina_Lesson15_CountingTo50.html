Tutor notes · how this lesson runs (≈45 min)
Zoom setup: you share the screen and click everything; Elina only talks. Tap ⭐ +1 in the top bar each time she says a full English sentence (it's the score for the whole lesson). Each slide shows its target minutes.
Pattern of the day: "There are + number + things." / "There is one ___." + counting by ones, tens and fives to 50. She already knows 1–30 (Lesson 3), so today pushes 30–50 and full sentences.
French-speaker traps: "There is twenty pencils" → There ARE twenty pencils (il y a = there is or there are); forgetting the plural -s ("twenty pencil"); "twenty-and-one" (from vingt et un) → twenty-one; "fivety" → fifty; thirTEEN vs THIRty stress (Sound spot).
Correcting: never "No." Repeat it back correctly ("Yes! There are forty books!") and let her say it again for the star.

Sound spot · -TEEN or -TY?3 min

👂 ThirTEEN or THIRty?

ThirTEEN ou THIRty ?
Listen for the strong part. In thir-TEEN, the end is long and loud: TEEEN. In THIR-ty, the start is strong and the end is short: -ty. 💪Écoute la partie forte. Dans « thir-TEEN », la fin est longue et forte : TEEEN. Dans « THIR-ty », le début est fort et la fin est courte : -ty. C'est comme ça qu'on entend 13 ou 30 !
