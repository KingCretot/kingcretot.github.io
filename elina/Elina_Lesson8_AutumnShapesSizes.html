Tutor notes · how this lesson runs (≈45 min)
Zoom setup: you share the screen and click everything; Elina only talks. Tap ⭐ +1 in the top bar each time she says a full English sentence (it's the score for the whole lesson). Each slide shows its target minutes.
Pattern of the day: "It's a + size + color + thing." → "It's a big orange pumpkin." + "What is it? What color is it? Is it big or small?"
French-speaker traps: French puts the color after the noun (un cercle orange); in English every describing word goes before the thing: a big orange circle. Order is size → color → thing. Describing words never take -s: "two big red apples" (not "bigs reds"). "a orange" → an orange, but a big orange pumpkin (big starts with a consonant). round (rond) describes; circle (un cercle) is the thing. American R in red, round, rectangle.
Correcting: never "No." Repeat it back correctly ("Yes! It's a big orange pumpkin!") and let her say it again for the star.
