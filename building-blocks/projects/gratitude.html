🫙 The Simon Family Gratitude Project

Monday, November 16 – Wednesday, December 2 · The girls present it at Lesson 12 on Thursday, December 3

What it is: For two and a half weeks, the whole family fills a Gratitude Jar. Every day, each person writes one thing they are thankful for, and the girls write slips for River and Cam too. The girls count and sort the slips (math), interview Mom and Dad (speaking and writing), write thank-you notes, and finish with a book (Bristol) and a poster (Bailey) to show at Lesson 12.

Time: about 20 minutes a day, plus 5 minutes at dinner for the jar. Thanksgiving week is lighter.

The Lesson 11 task list still counts. It is short (practice, one packet, reading). Do it alongside this project.

Supplies

  • A clean jar or box with a lid
  • Scissors, tape, markers or crayons
  • The printed worksheets (use the Print the worksheets button on the project page). Print the slips page 7 times (6 people × 14 days = 84 slips), or cut plain paper into strips.
  • Bristol: 4 sheets of plain paper, folded in half and stapled, for her book
  • Bailey: a poster board or 2 sheets of paper taped together
  • Envelopes for the thank-you notes

The jar rules (read these out loud on Day 1)

  1. Every day, each person writes one slip and puts it in the jar.
  2. Bristol writes one slip for River. Bailey writes one slip for Cam. Write what the dog would be thankful for: "River is thankful for…"
  3. Bristol's rule: one full sentence. Start with a capital letter. End with a period. Example: I am thankful for Dad because he makes pancakes.
  4. Bailey's rule: use the word because, and use one word from the Gratitude Word Bank at least every other day.
  5. At dinner, each person reads their slip out loud. Then it goes in the jar. Do not read old slips until Thanksgiving.

Gratitude Word Bank

Use one of these words in your slip at least every other day. Use 3 in your report and one in each thank-you note.

grateful: thankful for something
appreciate: to see the value in something and be glad for it
cherish: to love something and take good care of it
generous: happy to give or share
thoughtful: thinking about what other people need
fortunate: lucky
treasure: to value something a lot
acknowledge: to say out loud that something is true or that someone helped
