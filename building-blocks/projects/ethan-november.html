🍽️ The Ferguson Family Feast Planner

Thursday, November 12 – Tuesday, December 1 · Ethan presents it at Lesson 5 on Wednesday, December 2 (in person)

What it is: Ethan is the Head Planner for one special family meal: your Thanksgiving dinner on Thursday, November 26, or another special family dinner during the break. He plans the menu with the family, finds real store prices and keeps to a budget (money and decimals), makes a recipe big enough for everyone (multiplying and fractions), shares the leftovers fairly (division with remainders), runs a taste-test, and interviews Mom and Dad to compare their two viewpoints. He finishes with a Feast Report poster and presents it in 3 to 5 minutes at Lesson 5.

Time: 10 to 25 minutes a day for Ethan. Each day's time is shown in blue. A few days are off.

Why it helps: Lesson 5 is the Unit 2 Checkpoint. The Feast Math Warm-Up (page 4) reviews the same skills as Lessons 1 to 4.

Thu Nov 12–Tue Nov 17: finish Lesson 4's task list first; the project's daily part is short those days (10 minutes or less).

If your feast is on a different day, just move the Feast Day row to that day.

Supplies

  • The printed worksheets (use the Print the worksheets button on this page): 11 pages, printed once. Print page 3 (Price Hunt) twice if your menu has more than 10 items to buy.
  • A poster board, or 2 sheets of paper taped together
  • Markers or crayons, scissors, glue or tape
  • Measuring cups and spoons (for the recipe you make bigger)
  • A store, the store's website, or the weekly ad, to find real prices

The Head Planner rules

  1. Every price is a real price. Write it with a $ and two digits after the decimal point, like $3.49.
  2. Do the math by hand first. You can check it with a calculator after.
  3. Joyce and Clyde each pick one dish for the menu.
  4. When you interview someone, write their exact words in quotation marks. That is your evidence.

Scale It Up
Name
Date //

Pick one real recipe from your menu. Copy each ingredient. Then make it big enough for everyone.

Example: a recipe serves 4 and uses ¾ cup of milk. We need 8 servings, so we multiply by 2.   ¾ × 2 means ¾ + ¾ = 6/4 = 1 1/2 cups.

Recipe name: ______________________________

The recipe serves ______ people. We need ______ servings. ______ ÷ ______ = ______, so I multiply every amount by ______.

If the division does not come out even, round up. A little extra food is better than not enough! Fraction × whole number: ¾ × 2 means ¾ + ¾. Add the fraction that many times.

IngredientAmount in the recipe× ___New amount

Fraction check: find two amounts in your new recipe that use the same unit (like cups). Add them. If the denominators are different, find a common denominator first.
