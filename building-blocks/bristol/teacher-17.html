Teacher Plan · Lesson 17: Spelling Teams: oo

Monday, January 4, 2027 · Zoom · 60 minutes · Week 8: Welcome Back: Finish Spelling Teams: ea and oo, Then Super Sentences · Focus: Spelling patterns (ea, oo) (finish) + welcome-back review + Complete sentences, capitals, end marks

🎯 Objective: Bristol learns that the oo team says two sounds, the moon sound (moon, food, spoon, school, zoo) and the book sound (book, look, good, foot, cook, wood), and that both are spelled with two o's. She sorts oo words by sound, tells oo from a short o (hot, dog, box), and fixes her bock-for-book spelling. Week 7 Day 2 (Practice it). Her first lesson after the winter break, on Zoom: it opens with a short welcome back (her Holiday Gazette pages and a quick remember check of ea).
Student goal: I can spell words with oo, like moon and book.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 85

Materials

  • Zoom with screen share (open the Class lesson from the course page, code 9110)
  • At her home (ask Tia to write them before the lesson): 10 index cards: moon, food, pool, zoo, tooth, book, look, foot, wood, cook
  • At her home: paper or a small whiteboard and a marker; she holds her writing up to the camera. Your own whiteboard to hold up too.
  • A small mirror at her home (optional, for round lips on the moon sound)
  • Her Holiday Gazette pages (River's Diary and her Tray Math), next to the computer
  • Her book
  • Packet (2 pages) for Tia to print this week (it is on Wednesday's line of the task list)

Watch for

  • bock for book (her own mistake), lok for look, mon for moon: one o. Ask: "Moon sound or book sound? Then it needs two o's."
  • u for the oo sounds (fud, gud, skool, spune). Say: "This week, that sound is spelled oo."
  • school spelled skool or scool. It is a word to remember: s-c-h-o-o-l.
  • Sorting by looks instead of sound. Have her say each word next to moon and next to book.
  • Some words sound different in different places (roof, room, root, broom). We do not sort these by sound in this lesson. If she says a word another way, accept her way.
  • Words like door and floor if she finds them. Say: "That is a different sound. We learn it later."
