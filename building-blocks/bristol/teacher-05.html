Teacher Plan · Lesson 5: Prove It: 4s and 6s

Thursday, October 29, 2026 · In person · 60 minutes · Week 2: Facts for 4 and 6 · Focus: Facts 4, 6, 7, 8, 9

🎯 Objective: Bristol proves the Week 2 facts: mixed 4s (double, double) and 6s (5s + one more group), including turn-arounds; finds a missing factor (4 × ? = 24) by counting jumps or by guess, check and fix; and chooses multiply or add when two stories use the same numbers.
Student goal: I can solve 4s and 6s facts, find a missing number, and pick the right operation.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 85

Materials

  • About 50 counters or dried beans
  • 6 small cups or paper plates
  • Index cards: write 8 facts (4 × 6, 6 × 6, 6 × 8, 4 × 9, 6 × 4, 8 × 6, 4 × 7, 6 × 7) for a quick flash round
  • Whiteboard and marker
  • Her book, Amy and the Missing Puppy
  • Printed packet (2 pages) to send home

60-minute plan

0:00–0:07Book Talk
Slide 2. She retells the story so far with first, next, then (sequencing is her strength). Then push on the harder questions: the most important clue, and how Amy has changed.
0:07–0:13Flash round + Remember
Index cards: she says the trick, then the answer, for each card. Then slide 3. Note which facts she still has to think hard about.
0:13–0:25Hiding numbers (hands-on)
Give her 24 beans. Ask her to make groups of 4 in cups. How many cups? (6) Write 4 × 6 = 24. Then slides 4–6. On 6 × ? = 42, have her say each guess out loud and "too small" or "too big".
0:25–0:32Mixed facts
Slide 7 (sort). Point out that 4 × 9 and 6 × 6 are both 36, so the product alone does not tell you the fact.
0:32–0:43Same numbers, different job
Slides 8–10. Before tapping, she underlines (points to) each or every, or says it is missing. She builds 6 bowls of 8 on screen.
0:43–0:48Fix it
Slide 11. She explains each mistake in her own words before she taps.
0:48–0:56Show What You Know
Slide 12, on her own. No hints. She needs 85 (7 of 8) to open Lesson 6 on Monday.
0:56–1:00Wrap-up
Show the task list and the Times Table Trail link. Next week: 7s, 8s and 9s (the 8s use her 4s, so keep the 4s strong).

Watch for

  • Answering with the product instead of the hiding number (4 × ? = 24 → "24"). Ask: "How many jumps?"
  • Subtracting to find a hiding number (32 − 4 = 28). Have her check: does 4 × 28 make 32?
  • Multiplying any 4 and 6 she sees (Amy's 6 toys and Ellie's 4 toys = 24). Make her find the word each or every first.
  • Item 7 on the check (24 treats, 4 in each bag) is a hiding-number word problem. If she adds or subtracts, act it out with beans and cups.
