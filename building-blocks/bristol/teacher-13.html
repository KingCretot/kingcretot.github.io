Teacher Plan · Lesson 13: Contractions: The not Family

Monday, December 7, 2026 · Zoom · 60 minutes · Week 6: Contractions · Focus: Contractions

🎯 Objective: Bristol learns what a contraction is (two words pushed together; the apostrophe takes the place of the missing letters) and makes the not family: don't, isn't, aren't, wasn't, didn't, doesn't (the o drops out) and can't (the n and o drop out). She puts the apostrophe in the right spot and takes each contraction apart. Week 6 Day 1 (Learn it). won't waits for Thursday.
Student goal: I can push two words together to make a contraction, like don't and can't.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 85

Materials

  • At her home: 6 paper strips (or index cards cut in half), a marker, 4 sticky notes (or small paper squares), her book
  • Paper and pencil at her home for drawing an apostrophe and a comma
  • Packet (2 pages) for Tia to print this week (it is due Thursday, on Tuesday's task list)

Watch for

  • Putting the apostrophe before the n (did'nt, is'nt). Ask: "Which letter dropped out? Put the apostrophe where it was."
  • Putting the apostrophe at the end (dont'). Show the paper strip: the sticky note covers the o, in the middle.
  • Writing cann't or ca'nt for can't. Two letters drop out of not, so only one n is left.
  • Reading the contraction as the two long words (reading doesn't as "does not"). That is fine for meaning, but she should also say it as one word.
  • Mixing doesn't and don't ("we doesn't"). Say the long way: "we does not" sounds wrong, so it's don't.
  • Typed answers ignore the apostrophe (didnt is accepted on screen). When she spells out loud in class, YOU check the apostrophe spot. The packet and the multiple-choice items check the spot.
