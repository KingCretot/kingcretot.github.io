Teacher Plan · Lesson 3: Fours: Double, Double

Monday, October 26, 2026 · Zoom · 60 minutes · Week 2: Facts for 4 and 6 · Focus: Facts 4, 6, 7, 8, 9

🎯 Objective: Bristol learns ×4 as double, double: she finds the 2s fact (which she knows) and doubles it (4 × 7 → 14 → 28). She checks with an array split into two halves and with a number line. She keeps sorting equal groups (multiply) from unequal groups (add).
Student goal: I can find 4 times a number by doubling two times.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 85

Materials

  • Zoom with screen share (she taps on her own device)
  • At home: about 40 dried beans or counters, a sheet of paper, a pencil
  • Her book, Amy and the Missing Puppy
  • Printed packet (2 pages), printed by Tia before or after the lesson

60-minute plan

0:00–0:07Book Talk
Slide 2. She tells you the big problem and what Amy has tried. Ask her to predict what happens next and give a reason. Listen for a full sentence.
0:07–0:14Remember (spaced review)
Slide 3. Three review items from Week 1. Before she taps, she says out loud: equal groups or not?
0:14–0:244 is 2 + 2
Slides 4–5. Have her make 4 piles of 3 beans on camera. Push 2 piles to one side: 2 × 3 = 6. The other side is 6 too, so 6 + 6 = 12. Then the doubling warm-up; on double 18, she must say "regroup".
0:24–0:34Double, double
Slides 6–8. On the array, cover the bottom 2 rows with paper (have her do the same on her screen with her hand). Top half is 12, so all of it is 24. Number line: point to the halfway number (16).
0:34–0:44Build and practice
Slides 9–11. She builds 4 bowls of 4 on screen. Every time she taps an answer, she says the two doubles first ("9, 18, 36").
0:44–0:50Word problems
Slide 12. She reads each one aloud and says equal or not before she taps.
0:50–0:57Show What You Know
Slide 13, on her own. Turn off your camera feedback; no hints. She needs 85 (7 of 8) to open Lesson 4.
0:57–1:00Wrap-up
Share the course page. Show the task list and the Times Table Trail link. Tell her the 6s are tomorrow, and they use her 5s.

Watch for

  • Stopping after one double (4 × 9 = 18). Ask: "How many groups is that? 2 or 4?"
  • Forgetting to regroup when doubling 16 or 18 (writes 26 or 216). Have her line up tens and ones on paper.
  • Saying 4 × 8 = 24 because she counted 8, 16, 24 (three jumps). Have her count her jumps out loud on her fingers.
  • Multiplying any two numbers she sees (4 dogs and 6 dogs = 24). Make her say "each" or find that it is missing.

Linked KCE lessons
