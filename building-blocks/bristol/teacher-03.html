Teacher Plan · Lesson 3: Fours: Double, Double

Monday, October 26, 2026 · Zoom · 60 minutes · Week 2: Facts for 4 and 6 · Focus: Facts 4, 6, 7, 8, 9

🎯 Objective: Bristol learns ×4 as double, double: she finds the 2s fact (which she knows) and doubles it (4 × 7 → 14 → 28). She checks with an array split into two halves and with a number line. She keeps sorting equal groups (multiply) from unequal groups (add).
Student goal: I can find 4 times a number by doubling two times.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 85

Materials

  • Zoom with screen share: share the class lesson page (code 9110)
  • At home (ask Tia the day before): about 40 dried beans or cereal pieces, a sheet of paper, a pencil, a folded paper card to cover rows
  • Her book, Amy and the Missing Puppy
  • Lesson 3 packet: printed later in the week (it is on Tuesday's task list)

60-minute plan

0:00–0:08Hello and Book Talk
Class slides 1–2. Check her materials on camera. She tells the big problem, what Amy has tried, and predicts with a reason.
0:08–0:12Remember (Week 1)
Class slide 3. She says equal groups or not before each answer; you type or tap for her.
0:12–0:19Doubles
Class slides 4–5. Two piles of 3 beans on camera = double 3. Doubling warm-up; on double 18 she must say "regroup".
0:19–0:304 is 2 + 2 (beans)
Class slides 6–8. 4 piles of 3: push 2 aside, 6 + 6 = 12. Then 4 rows of 6 on paper, cover 2 rows with the card: 12 and 12. Then 4 × 5 by double, double, which matches her 5s.
0:30–0:37Number line, steps, build
Class slides 9–11. Count the jumps on fingers (halfway is 16). Order the steps for 4 × 7. She makes 4 piles of 4 beans and tells you the buttons.
0:37–0:47Practice and turn-arounds
Class slides 12–14. She says both doubles before every answer. Turn-around: 7 × 4 = 4 × 7. Sort: she names the box, you tap.
0:47–0:56Word problems and paper
Class slides 15–16. Equal or not before each answer. Then 4 facts on paper with both doubles, held up to the camera.
0:56–1:00Wrap-up
Class slide 17. Share the course page: tonight only the after-class lesson (ends with Show What You Know) and 10 minutes of reading. Tomorrow in person: the 6s.

Watch for

  • Stopping after one double (4 × 9 = 18). Ask: "How many groups is that? 2 or 4?"
  • Forgetting to regroup when doubling 16 or 18 (writes 26 or 216). Have her line up tens and ones on paper.
  • Saying 4 × 8 = 24 because she counted 8, 16, 24 (three jumps). Have her count her jumps out loud on her fingers.
  • Multiplying any two numbers she sees (4 dogs and 6 dogs = 24). Make her say "each" or find that it is missing.
