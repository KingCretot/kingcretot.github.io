Teacher Plan · Lesson 6: Tricks for 8s and 9s

Monday, November 2, 2026 · Zoom · 60 minutes · Week 3: Facts for 7, 8 and 9 · Focus: Facts 4, 6, 7, 8, 9

🎯 Objective: Bristol finds 8s facts by doubling the 4s fact (8 × 6: 4 × 6 = 24, double to 48) and 9s facts by taking one group away from the 10s fact (9 × 7: 70 − 7 = 63). Both tricks build on facts she owns, so she stops skip-counting from zero.
Student goal: I can use my 4s to find 8s, and my 10s to find 9s.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 85

Materials

  • Zoom with screen share; your whiteboard on camera
  • At home: about 40 beans or counters, paper and pencil
  • Her book, Amy and the Missing Puppy
  • Packet (2 pages) printed at home

60-minute plan

0:00–0:07Book Talk
Slide 2. Ask who the main character is, what her problem is, and what she tried. Push for a full sentence with because.
0:07–0:13Warm-up: 4s and 6s
Slide 3. Before she taps, she says the trick out loud: 4s = double double, 6s = 5s plus one group.
0:13–0:288 = double the 4s
Slides 4–6. On camera, she makes 4 rows of 3 beans, then 4 more rows. Ask: what did the 4 extra rows do to the total? On slide 5 she doubles 24 out loud: tens first, then ones.
0:28–0:429 = 10s take away one group
Slides 7–9. She makes 10 rows of 4 beans, then pushes one row away. Ask: how many did you take away, 1 or 4? Then 9 × 7 on the whiteboard.
0:42–0:50Sort, build, word problems
Slides 10–12. In the sort she says which trick before she taps. On slide 12 she reads each problem aloud and says equal groups or not first.
0:50–0:57Show What You Know
Slide 13, on her own. Camera on, hands off. She needs 85 (7 of 8) to open Lesson 7.
0:57–1:00Wrap-up
Show the task list. Practice and both packet pages are due before tomorrow's lesson.

Watch for

  • For 9s, taking away 9 or 1 instead of one group (9 × 6 = 51 or 59). Ask: what is in one group?
  • For 8s, adding 4 instead of doubling (8 × 7 = 32). Ask: how many groups of 7 did we have? How many do we need?
  • Doubling 2-digit numbers: 28 + 28 = 46 (forgot to regroup). Have her double tens, then ones, then add.
  • Saying 7 × 8 = 54. Fix it now: 4 × 7 = 28, double is 56.
