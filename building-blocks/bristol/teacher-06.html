Teacher Plan · Lesson 6: Tricks for 8s and 9s

Monday, November 2, 2026 · Zoom · 60 minutes · Week 3: Facts for 7, 8 and 9 · Focus: Facts 4, 6, 7, 8, 9

🎯 Objective: Bristol finds 8s facts by doubling the 4s fact (8 × 6: 4 × 6 = 24, double to 48) and 9s facts by taking one group away from the 10s fact (9 × 7: 70 − 7 = 63). Both tricks build on facts she owns, so she stops skip-counting from zero.
Student goal: I can use my 4s to find 8s, and my 10s to find 9s.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 85

Materials

  • Zoom with screen share (open the Class lesson and type the teacher code); your whiteboard on camera
  • At home: about 40 beans or counters, paper and pencil
  • Her book, Amy and the Missing Puppy
  • Packet (2 pages): you send it home after Tuesday's lesson, with the Lesson 7 packet

60-minute plan

0:00–0:03Hello and set-up
Class slide 1. Check she has beans, paper, pencil and her book on camera. She names the facts she knows fast.
0:03–0:10Book Talk
Class slide 2. Main character, problem, what she tried. Push for a full sentence with because.
0:10–0:15Warm-up: 4s and 6s
Class slide 3. She says the trick out loud before each answer; you tap what she says.
0:15–0:318 = double the 4s
Class slides 4–8. Bean rows on camera: 4 rows of 3, then 4 more rows. Quick check (slide 5): she says, you type. Whiteboard: double 24 with tens then ones (slide 6), then she doubles 16, 28 and 36 (slide 7) and taps the 8s facts (slide 8).
0:31–0:439 = 10s take away one group
Class slides 9–11. She builds 10 rows of 4 and pushes one row away. Ask: 1 or 4? Then 9 × 7 on the whiteboard and three taps.
0:43–0:50Which trick?
Class slides 12–14. Which trick? (she says double or take away before she answers), build 9 pots of 3, then the call-out: she says the trick and the answer before you click.
0:50–0:56Word problems
Class slide 15. She reads aloud and says equal groups or not before she answers.
0:56–1:00Wrap-up
Class slide 16. She says both tricks. Show the task list: tonight only the after-class lesson (with Show What You Know) and 10 minutes of reading.

Watch for

  • For 9s, taking away 9 or 1 instead of one group (9 × 6 = 51 or 59). Ask: what is in one group?
  • For 8s, adding 4 instead of doubling (8 × 7 = 32). Ask: how many groups of 7 did we have? How many do we need?
  • Doubling 2-digit numbers: 28 + 28 = 46 (forgot to regroup). Have her double tens, then ones, then add.
  • Saying 7 × 8 = 54. Fix it now: 4 × 7 = 28, double is 56.
