Teacher Plan · Lesson 2: Bigger or Smaller? Theo’s Big Problem

Tuesday, November 3, 2026 · In person · 60 minutes · Week 2: Comparing Numbers and Plot · Focus: Comparing and ordering numbers + plot and conflict (Pathways Week 2)

🎯 Objective: Math (about 34 min): Ce'Line compares two 3-digit numbers by lining up the places and starting at the hundreds (a tie means move one place right), writes and reads the symbols <, > and = as "less than", "greater than" and "equal to", orders three or four numbers from least to greatest and greatest to least, and reads whether the bigger or the smaller number wins (race times, lightest pack). Reading (about 26 min, with the book talk): in Theo's First Hike (Pathways Week 2 Reading) she names the conflict, Theo's attempt and the resolution, links them with cause and effect, and types conflict, attempt and resolution from memory. A short warm-up reviews Week 1 (place value, rounding, setting).
Student goal: I can compare and order numbers, and I can find the problem, the try and the fix in a story.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 85

Watch for

  • Starting at the wrong end: if she says 389 is bigger than 463 "because 8 and 9 are big," she is comparing digits, not places. Cover the tens and ones with a card and compare only the hundreds first.
  • Stopping at a tie: for 536 vs 532 she may say "they're the same" after the hundreds. Ask: did EVERY place tie? Slide to the right one place at a time.
  • Flipping the symbol: the open side faces the bigger number. Have her read every symbol as words ("is less than"); a symbol that reads wrong out loud is easy for her to catch.
  • Same digits, different places: 409/490/904/940 and 362/623 use the same digits. Point at each digit and ask what it is worth (Week 1 place value).
  • Least vs greatest: she circles least or greatest before ordering. Typed lists need commas or spaces between the numbers ("418, 481, 841").
  • Smaller wins: in races, a smaller time is faster. Before each word problem, she says out loud whether the bigger or the smaller number wins.
  • Conflict vs resolution: she may call the whole story "the problem." Use the three index cards: problem (conflict), try (attempt), fix (resolution). A first attempt can fail (Tess, Ivy, Rosa, Kai, Jay).
  • Confidence: she needs a lot of support and a lot of wins. Praise every correct step by name, read hard words with her, and let her answer out loud before she types.
