Teacher Plan · Lesson 4: Solve It, Estimate It and The Last S'more

Tuesday, December 1, 2026 · In person · 60 minutes · Week 4: Word Problems and Theme · Focus: Word problems and estimation + theme and lesson of a story (Pathways Week 4)

🎯 Objective: Ce'Line comes back from the three-week break with a quick review of Weeks 1–3 (rounding, comparing, regrouping; setting, conflict and resolution, sequence). Then she solves one-step addition and subtraction word problems by finding the clue word (in all, altogether, total, both / left, remain, how many more, difference), estimates sums and differences by rounding to the nearest hundred, and uses the estimate to decide if an answer is reasonable. In reading, she reads “The Last S'more” (Pathways Week 4), infers Mia's feelings from clues, tells plot from theme, and states the theme of a story in a whole sentence.
Student goal: I can find the clue word, add or subtract, estimate to check, and tell the lesson a story teaches.

Watch for

  • Picking the operation from the numbers, not the words: she may add every time. Before she writes anything, she puts her finger on the clue word and says “add” or “subtract.” No clue word said, no pencil.
  • “How many more”: the word “more” pulls students toward adding. Show the gap with fingers (5 and 8: 3 more) and write 8 − 5.
  • Borrowing: when the top digit is smaller (2 − 8), the common mistake is to flip it (8 − 2). Say: “Top digit too small? Borrow.” With zeros (400 − 162), borrow from the hundreds first.
  • Rounding to the wrong place: she learned tens in Week 1. Today is hundreds: she circles the tens digit, then decides up or down.
  • Plot vs. theme: she may retell what happened. Ask: “Does it name Theo? Then it is plot. What does it teach YOU?”
  • One-word themes: “kindness” is the key word, but on the packet she writes a whole sentence (the Pathways retake asks for the key word, and both are right).
  • Confidence after the break: praise every correct step out loud. If she freezes, give the first step and let her finish.
