Teacher Plan · Lesson 7: Pricing Day and The Grumpy Hiker

Tuesday, December 29, 2026 · In person · 60 minutes · Week 7: Pricing Day and The Grumpy Hiker · Focus: Pathways Week 7 math (Pricing Day at the Trading Post) and reading (The Grumpy Hiker)

🎯 Objective: Ce'Line compares two characters' points of view on the same moment in The Grumpy Hiker: Theo felt hurt and thought the hiker was rude; Mia noticed the muddy boots, the torn strap and the empty water bottle and inferred he was exhausted. She explains that both views can be real, uses clues to infer how a character feels in new stories, and types the Week 7 words (point of view, perspective, empathy, interpret). In math she skip-counts by 5s and 10s (up to 100), knows that multiples of 5 end in 0 or 5 and multiples of 10 end in 0, connects the number of jumps to a times fact (9 jumps of 5 = 9 × 5 = 45), and solves pricing word problems with price × quantity = total, choosing multiply only when every item has the same price.
Student goal: I can skip-count by 5s and 10s to find price totals, and I can compare two characters' points of view.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 85

Watch for

  • Theo vs. Mia: Pathways asks who felt hurt (Theo), who noticed the clues (Mia), what Theo thought the hiker was (rude) and what Mia inferred (exhausted). Say: Theo felt it, Mia saw it.
  • 'Theo was wrong': Pathways wants 'his hurt feelings were still real'. If she says Theo was wrong, ask: did Theo really feel hurt? Then both views are real.
  • Inferring vs. guessing: every feeling she names must come with a clue from the story. If she says 'he was sad', ask: which clue tells you that?
  • Whose view? In the new stories (Kiko and Grandpa), she may give the same feeling to both people. Ask: what does EACH person care about?
  • Spelling: exhausted, empathy, perspective and interpret are typed in Pathways. Watch for exausted, empethy, prespective.
  • Adding the price and the quantity: $5 and 6 pairs → 11 means she added. Lay down one $5 slip for each pair and skip-count.
  • Skip-count slips: if she adds 10 when counting by 5s (55, 60, 65, 75), have her touch one slip per jump and say each number.
  • Spot the mistake: in a skip-count list, check every jump. By 5s, every number ends in 0 or 5. By 10s, every number ends in 0. 21 or 45 in the wrong list is the mistake.
  • Multiples: multiples of 5 end in 0 or 5; multiples of 10 end in 0. 35 is a multiple of 5 but not of 10.
  • Not every money problem is multiply: 'left' and 'how much more' mean subtract; two different prices mean add. Same price for each item means multiply.
