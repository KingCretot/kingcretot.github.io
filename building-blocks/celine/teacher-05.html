Watch for

  • Confidence first: she has struggled on Pathways, so praise each correct column and each correct story answer out loud. If she freezes, do the first step together and let her finish.
  • Rounding (Week 1): to the nearest hundred, look at the TENS digit; to the nearest ten, look at the ONES digit. The usual slip is looking at the wrong digit (765 → she says 770 instead of 800 when asked for the nearest hundred).
  • Comparing (Week 2): hundreds first, then tens. Watch the direction of > and <: the open side faces the bigger number. In races and times, the SMALLEST number wins.
  • Regrouping (Week 3): she may forget to add the carried 1, or flip the digits when subtracting (1 − 6 written as 6 − 1, so 451 − 176 becomes 325). A 0 in the tens (500 − 237, 700 − 346) means borrowing from the hundreds first. Have her check subtraction by adding.
  • Word problems (Week 4): “in all, altogether, total” = add; “left, remain, how many more, difference” = subtract. “More” alone is not always add. On estimate questions she must add the ROUNDED numbers (the Checkpoint asks for 700 for 264 + 419, not 683).
  • Reading: conflict = the problem, attempt = a try, resolution = the fix. Theme is a lesson about life, not an event; a theme must match what happens. The Checkpoint asks many questions about Theo's stories, so the story talk matters.
  • On the Checkpoint: 2 tries per question, and only the first try earns points. It wants single words for the vocabulary blanks (regroup, borrow, carry, estimate, theme, conflict, sequence, plot, resolution, infer). She works every math problem on paper and compares her paper to the screen before pressing Check.
