Teacher Plan · Lesson 6: Stocking the Shelves and the Storm

Tuesday, December 15, 2026 · In person · 60 minutes · Week 6: Stocking the Shelves · Focus: Pathways Week 6 math and reading (The Storm in the Stockroom)

🎯 Objective: Ce'Line traces a cause-and-effect chain in a story: she finds the cause (why) and the effect (what happened), even when the cause comes after 'because', puts Mia's storm chain in order from the loose shutter to less inventory for opening day, and names middle events as both a cause and an effect. In math she connects equal groups, repeated addition and multiplication (4 groups of 5 = 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 = 4 × 5 = 20), reads arrays as rows × how many in each row, tells equal from unequal groups (multiply vs. add), and types the 10 Pathways Week 6 words (cause, effect, chain reaction, consequence, trace, multiply, equal groups, repeated addition, array, product).
Student goal: I can use equal groups, arrays and repeated addition to multiply, and I can trace a chain of causes and effects in a story.

Watch for

  • Cause after 'because': in 'The milk spilled because the cup tipped over', the cause is the SECOND part. If she picks whatever comes first in the sentence, ask: which happened first in time?
  • First cause in Mia's story: Pathways wants 'a loose shutter'. If she says 'the puddles' (what Mia saw first) or 'rain got inside', trace it back with the index cards.
  • Middle events: she may call a middle event only a cause or only an effect. Have her ask both questions: what caused it? what did it cause? If both have answers, it is both.
  • Mia and Theo: Pathways asks who led the investigation (Mia) and who boarded up the shutter (Theo). Say: Mia found it, Theo fixed it.
  • Adding the factors: 3 × 4 = 7 means she added the two numbers. Build it with cups and counters: 3 cups, 4 in each.
  • Counting by ones: if she counts every counter one by one, model the skip count once (3, 6, 9 …) and have her touch each cup as she says it.
  • Groups vs. how many in each: for 6 + 6 + 6 she should say 'three 6s, 3 × 6'. 6 × 3 is accepted everywhere (same product), but the words 'groups of' must match.
  • Unequal groups: if she multiplies when the bags have different numbers (Jo, Theo, Rae), ask: does every bag have the same number? If not, add.
  • Arrays: rows go across. If she counts columns as rows, run a finger along one row and say 'this is one row'.
  • Typing words: product, array, consequence and trace are typed in Pathways. Watch spelling (prodct, arry, consequense).
