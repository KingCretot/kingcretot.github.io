Teacher Plan · Lesson 7: Bigger Boxes: Estimate, Then Multiply

Tuesday, November 3, 2026 · In person · 60 minutes · Week 3: The Area Model: Stop Adding, Start Multiplying · Focus: Area model / choosing the efficient method

🎯 Objective: Bailey estimates a product first (round down and up to whole hundreds or tens), then uses a three-box area model for 3-digit × 1-digit and a four-box area model for 2-digit × 2-digit (23 × 14 = 200 + 30 + 80 + 12). She uses the estimate to judge whether her answer is reasonable.
Student goal: I can estimate first, then use an area model for bigger numbers like 247 × 6 and 23 × 14.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 90

Materials

  • Grid paper (1 cm squares), scissors and 2 colored pencils
  • Base-ten blocks if you have them (hundreds flats, tens rods, ones)
  • Whiteboard and marker
  • Her book, How to Train Your Dragon
  • Last night's packet from Lesson 6 to check together
  • Printed packet (2 pages)

60-minute plan

0:00–0:06Book Talk
Slide 2. She uses a new word (crafty or fearsome) about a real character and proves it with an event.
0:06–0:12Word Power + packet check
Slide 3. Flip all 7 cards. Then 2 minutes on last night's Lesson 6 packet: check one area model together.
0:12–0:20Estimate first
Slides 4–5. On the whiteboard: 247 is between 200 and 300, so the answer is between 1,200 and 1,800. She says the estimate aloud before tapping each item.
0:20–0:28Three boxes
Slides 6–7. If you have base-ten blocks, build 247 as 2 flats, 4 rods, 7 ones, then make 6 copies of each kind to see 1,200 + 240 + 42. She writes the three partial products on paper before tapping.
0:28–0:42Four boxes (hands-on)
Before slide 8: she draws a 23 by 14 rectangle on grid paper, cuts it along the 20/3 and 10/4 lines into 4 pieces, and counts each piece (200, 30, 80, 12). Then slides 8–10. In the sort she says column × row for each card.
0:42–0:47Recall + Word Power
Slides 11–12. Quick and out loud.
0:47–0:56Show What You Know
Slide 13 on her own, scratch paper allowed. One try each. She needs 90 (9 of 10) to open Lesson 8.
0:56–1:00Wrap-up
Show the task list. Packet page 1 on Wednesday, page 2 before Thursday.

Watch for

  • Doing only two of the four boxes (tens × tens and ones × ones: 16 × 24 = 200 + 24). Have her count boxes before adding; the grid-paper cutout makes the missing pieces visible.
  • Hundreds with too few zeros (300 × 7 = 210). Say it in words: 3 hundreds × 7 = 21 hundreds.
  • Picking the most complicated route and losing track. Keep the boxes labeled; she writes each partial product inside its box before adding.
  • Skipping the estimate. Do not let her start multiplying until the between-numbers are written down.
  • Losing a regroup when adding three or four partial products. Have her add them in a column, lined up by place.
