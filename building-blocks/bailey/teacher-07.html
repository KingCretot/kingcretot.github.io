Teacher Plan · Lesson 7: Bigger Boxes: Estimate, Then Multiply

Tuesday, November 3, 2026 · In person · 60 minutes · Week 3: The Area Model: Stop Adding, Start Multiplying · Focus: Area model / choosing the efficient method

🎯 Objective: Bailey estimates a product first (round down and up to whole hundreds or tens), then uses a three-box area model for 3-digit × 1-digit and a four-box area model for 2-digit × 2-digit (23 × 14 = 200 + 30 + 80 + 12). She uses the estimate to judge whether her answer is reasonable.
Student goal: I can estimate first, then use an area model for bigger numbers like 247 × 6 and 23 × 14.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 90

Watch for

  • Doing only two of the four boxes (tens × tens and ones × ones: 16 × 24 = 200 + 24). Have her count boxes before adding; the grid-paper cutout makes the missing pieces visible.
  • Hundreds with too few zeros (300 × 7 = 210). Say it in words: 3 hundreds × 7 = 21 hundreds.
  • Picking the most complicated route and losing track. Keep the boxes labeled; she writes each partial product inside its box before adding.
  • Skipping the estimate. Do not let her start multiplying until the between-numbers are written down.
  • Losing a regroup when adding three or four partial products. Have her add them in a column, lined up by place.
