Teacher Plan · Lesson 7: Bigger Boxes: Estimate, Then Multiply

Saturday, November 7, 2026 · Zoom · 60 minutes · Week 3: The Area Model: Stop Adding, Start Multiplying · Focus: Area model / choosing the efficient method

🎯 Objective: Bailey estimates a product first (round down and up to whole hundreds or tens), then uses a three-box area model for 3-digit × 1-digit and a four-box area model for 2-digit × 2-digit (23 × 14 = 200 + 30 + 80 + 12). She uses the estimate to judge whether her answer is reasonable. It is on Zoom: she writes and types nothing in class; she says every answer and choice out loud.
Student goal: I can estimate first, then use an area model for bigger numbers like 247 × 6 and 23 × 14.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 90

Materials

  • Zoom with screen share (open the Class Lesson; teacher code 9110)
  • On your side, in camera view: 1-cm grid paper, scissors and 2 colored pencils (you draw and cut; she tells you what to do)
  • Base-ten blocks if you have them (hundreds flats, tens rods, ones), in camera view; otherwise quick sketches on your whiteboard
  • Your whiteboard and marker, on camera
  • Her book, How to Train Your Dragon (next to her)
  • Her Lesson 6 score from last Tuesday night (Formspree)
  • Packet (2 pages): both pages are on Wednesday's task list

Watch for

  • Doing only two of the four boxes (tens × tens and ones × ones: 16 × 24 = 200 + 24). Have her count boxes before adding; the grid-paper cutout makes the missing pieces visible.
  • Hundreds with too few zeros (300 × 7 = 210). Say it in words: 3 hundreds × 7 = 21 hundreds.
  • Picking the most complicated route and losing track. Keep the boxes labeled; she says each partial product and you write it inside its box before adding.
  • Skipping the estimate. Do not let her start multiplying until the between-numbers are written down.
  • Losing a regroup when adding three or four partial products. Write them in a column, lined up by place, as she says them.
