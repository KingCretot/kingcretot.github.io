Teacher Plan · Lesson 11: Share or Group? Prove It!

Thursday, November 12, 2026 · In person · 60 minutes · Week 4: Division Undoes Multiplication · Focus: Division facts and fact families

🎯 Objective: Bailey proves the week: she interprets division stories as sharing (find how many in each) or grouping (find how many groups), finds unknowns in any spot of a fact family (? ÷ 8 = 9, 63 ÷ ? = 9), solves mixed 6–9 division facts, and confirms every answer by multiplying (quotient × divisor + remainder = dividend).
Student goal: I can tell if a story is sharing or grouping, find the missing number, and confirm my answer by multiplying.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 90

Watch for

  • Adding or subtracting the two numbers in a story (63 − 9 = 54) instead of dividing. Ask: is the total being split up? Then it is division.
  • Mixing up the label: answering "8 groups of dragons" when the story asks how many fish each dragon gets. Ask: what does your answer count?
  • For ? ÷ 8 = 9, adding 8 + 9 = 17. Ask: which number is missing, and which is the biggest number in this family? 9 × 8 = 72.
  • Confirming with the wrong operation, or forgetting to add the remainder when checking (6 × 7 + 2). Point to the whiteboard rule.
  • Picking a long method (drawing 72 dots) when a fact works. Praise the drawing once, then ask: which fact gives this faster?
  • "I can't do it" on a word problem. Say: "It looks daunting. What is the first detective step?" and wait.
