Teacher Plan · Lesson 11: Share or Group? Prove It!

Thursday, November 12, 2026 · In person · 60 minutes · Week 4: Division Undoes Multiplication · Focus: Division facts and fact families

🎯 Objective: Bailey proves the week: she interprets division stories as sharing (find how many in each) or grouping (find how many groups), finds unknowns in any spot of a fact family (? ÷ 8 = 9, 63 ÷ ? = 9), solves mixed 6–9 division facts, and confirms every answer by multiplying (quotient × divisor + remainder = dividend).
Student goal: I can tell if a story is sharing or grouping, find the missing number, and confirm my answer by multiplying.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 90

Materials

  • About 60 dried beans or counters
  • 8 small cups or paper plates
  • Whiteboard and marker
  • Her fact family index cards from Lessons 9 and 10
  • 6 sticky notes labeled Sharing or Grouping (3 of each)
  • Her book, How to Train Your Dragon
  • Printed packet (2 pages)

60-minute plan

0:00–0:07Book Talk
Slides 1–2. Focus on conflict and how Hiccup solves problems with his brain, not strength. She connects one of this week's words to a moment in the book and explains why it fits.
0:07–0:11Warm-up review
Slide 3. She explains each answer out loud, including why the remainder is smaller than the divisor.
0:11–0:17Word Power
Slide 4. She reads each card and uses it to describe something from this week. Make sure she says daunting about a problem, then says what she does about it.
0:17–0:27Sharing or grouping (hands-on)
Slides 5–6. With beans: 42 beans shared onto 7 plates, then 42 beans in plates of 7. She puts a Sharing or Grouping sticky note on each setup. Then she acts out two sort cards with beans before sorting the rest. Ask each time: what does the other number tell you?
0:27–0:36Confirm it
Slides 7–8. On the whiteboard write quotient × divisor (+ remainder) = dividend. She confirms each answer out loud before tapping.
0:36–0:43Unknowns and detective steps
Slides 9–10. For ? ÷ 8 = 9, ask: which number is missing, the dividend, the divisor or the quotient? Then the routine in order.
0:43–0:48Story problems and words
Slides 11–12. She uses the detective steps on each story: interpret, divide, confirm, label.
0:48–0:57Show What You Know
Slide 13, on her own. One try each. She needs 90 (9 of 10) to open Lesson 12.
0:57–1:00Wrap-up
Show the task list and the Division Station Lesson 5 link. Explain the break: the next lesson is Lesson 12, a welcome-back review, so she keeps her fact cards handy.

Watch for

  • Adding or subtracting the two numbers in a story (63 − 9 = 54) instead of dividing. Ask: is the total being split up? Then it is division.
  • Mixing up the label: answering "8 groups of dragons" when the story asks how many fish each dragon gets. Ask: what does your answer count?
  • For ? ÷ 8 = 9, adding 8 + 9 = 17. Ask: which number is missing, and which is the biggest number in this family? 9 × 8 = 72.
  • Confirming with the wrong operation, or forgetting to add the remainder when checking (6 × 7 + 2). Point to the whiteboard rule.
  • Picking a long method (drawing 72 dots) when a fact works. Praise the drawing once, then ask: which fact gives this faster?
  • "I can't do it" on a word problem. Say: "It looks daunting. What is the first detective step?" and wait.
