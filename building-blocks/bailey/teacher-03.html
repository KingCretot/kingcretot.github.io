Teacher Plan · Lesson 3: Double, Double, Double: The 8s

Monday, October 26, 2026 · Zoom · 60 minutes · Week 2: Fact Shortcuts: x8 and x7 · Focus: Facts 6–9 automatic + fact strategies

🎯 Objective: Bailey finds ×4 facts by doubling twice and ×8 facts by doubling three times (8 × 7: 14, 28, 56), so she stops adding 8 over and over and losing count. She also learns seven new words (double, halve, quadruple, hoard, gargantuan, formidable, amplify).
Student goal: I can find any ×8 fact by doubling three times.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 90

Materials

  • Zoom with screen share; Bailey opens the lesson on her own device and taps
  • Her book, How to Train Your Dragon
  • Printed packet (2 pages) ready at home for after the lesson
  • Your own whiteboard on camera to model doubling chains

60-minute plan

0:00–0:07Book Talk
Slide 2. She retells in order, names Hiccup's biggest problem and the type of conflict, and says what Hiccup uses instead of muscles (brains, planning, watching). Connect it: today we use a smart tool instead of brute force.
0:07–0:12Recall: ×9 and ×6
Slide 3. She says the shortcut out loud before tapping ("60 take away 6"). If she starts adding 9s, stop her and ask, "What is 10 groups?"
0:12–0:22Word Power
Slides 4–5. She flips each card, reads it aloud, and makes her own sentence. Push for book-based sentences (a gargantuan dragon, a formidable chief). Ask her to explain how quadruple and double are related.
0:22–0:34×4 and ×8 by doubling
Slides 6–8. Model on your whiteboard: 6 → 12 → 24. She counts doubles on her fingers out loud. On slide 8, she says every double before you reveal the next step.
0:34–0:44Build, order, sort
Slides 9–11. In the build, ask: what are the top 4 nests? Then why can we just double 24? In the sort, make her say the turn-around for 7 × 4 and 5 × 8.
0:44–0:49Doubling on Berk
Slide 12. She reads each problem aloud and names the equal groups before she taps. For Fishlegs' mistake, have her fix it out loud.
0:49–0:57Show What You Know
Slide 13 on her own. Camera on, hands off. One try each. She needs 90 (9 of 10) to open Lesson 4.
0:57–1:00Wrap-up
Show the task list on the course page. Packet and Practice are due before tomorrow's in-person lesson.

Watch for

  • Adding the start number instead of doubling (7, 14, 21, 28). Say: "Double means add the number to itself. What is 14 + 14?"
  • Losing count of the doubles. Have her hold up one finger per double and stop at three.
  • Doubling a 2-digit number (28 + 28) slowly. Break it: 20 + 20 = 40, 8 + 8 = 16, 40 + 16 = 56.
  • "I can't do it." Point back to the chain: "You only need three steps. Do the first one."
  • Mixing up quadruple (×4) and the ×8 fact. Ask: how many doubles is quadruple?
