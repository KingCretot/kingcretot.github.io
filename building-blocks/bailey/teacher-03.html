Teacher Plan · Lesson 3: Double, Double, Double: The 8s

Monday, October 26, 2026 · Zoom · 60 minutes · Week 2: Fact Shortcuts: x8 and x7 · Focus: Facts 6–9 automatic + fact strategies

🎯 Objective: Bailey finds ×4 facts by doubling twice and ×8 facts by doubling three times (8 × 7: 14, 28, 56), so she stops adding 8 over and over and losing count. She also learns seven new words (double, halve, quadruple, hoard, gargantuan, formidable, amplify).
Student goal: I can find any ×8 fact by doubling three times.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 90

Materials

  • Zoom with screen share: you share this class lesson; she answers out loud and you tap or type her answer (or give her remote control)
  • At home: about 60 dried beans (or cereal pieces), a sheet of paper, a pencil, and paper or a small whiteboard to hold up
  • Her book, How to Train Your Dragon
  • Your own whiteboard on camera to model doubling chains

60-minute plan

0:00–0:03Hello + set-up
Class slide 1. Share your screen. Check she has beans, paper and pencil. Quick warm-up: 7 + 7 and 14 + 14.
0:03–0:10Book Talk
Class slide 2. She retells in order and names Hiccup's biggest problem and the type of conflict.
0:10–0:14Recall: ×9 and ×6
Class slide 3. She says the shortcut out loud before each answer.
0:14–0:24Word Power
Class slides 4–5. Teach the 7 words one at a time; she writes each on paper and makes a book sentence. Slide 5: she says why the wrong choices are wrong.
0:24–0:31×4 = double, double (beans)
Class slides 6–7. At home she builds 1 row of 6 beans, doubles it, doubles again, and holds it up. She says each double out loud.
0:31–0:38×8 = three doubles
Class slides 8–9. She says each double before you reveal it. Practice big doubles (24 + 24, 32 + 32) by tens and ones on paper.
0:38–0:44Build and order
Class slides 10–11. 8 piles of 6 beans at home; top half is 4 × 6, so double 24. Then she orders the 8 × 9 chain aloud.
0:44–0:50How many doubles + Berk problems
Class slides 12–13. Fingers up for the number of doubles before typing. She writes each chain on paper before tapping.
0:50–0:56Teach it back
Class slide 14. She explains why three doubles is ×8, makes up an 8 × 4 dragon problem, and halves 8 × 7.
0:56–1:00Wrap-up
Class slide 15. Show the task list: tonight is only the After-Class Lesson (with Show What You Know) and 10 minutes of reading.

Watch for

  • Adding the start number instead of doubling (7, 14, 21, 28). Say: "Double means add the number to itself. What is 14 + 14?"
  • Losing count of the doubles. Have her hold up one finger per double and stop at three.
  • Doubling a 2-digit number (28 + 28) slowly. Break it: 20 + 20 = 40, 8 + 8 = 16, 40 + 16 = 56.
  • "I can't do it." Point back to the chain: "You only need three steps. Do the first one."
  • Mixing up quadruple (×4) and the ×8 fact. Ask: how many doubles is quadruple?
