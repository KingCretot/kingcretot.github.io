Teacher Plan · Lesson 14: Bigger Numbers, Same Four Steps

Tuesday, December 8, 2026 · In person · 60 minutes · Week 6: Long Division, One Step at a Time · Focus: Long division, 1-digit divisors

🎯 Objective: Bailey extends long division to 3-digit ÷ 1-digit with no remainder: hundreds first with an estimate (between 100 and 200), a placeholder zero when the divisor does not fit (618 ÷ 6 = 103), and a first digit too small to divide, which gives a 2-digit quotient (184 ÷ 8 = 23). She checks each answer by multiplying.
Student goal: I can use long division to divide a 3-digit number by a 1-digit number, even when the quotient needs a zero.

Watch for

  • Omitting the placeholder zero (618 ÷ 6 = 13). Ask: "Did you estimate? About how big should it be?" Then: "How many tens does each plate get?"
  • Writing a quotient digit above the first digit when the divisor does not fit (putting 0 or 2 above the 1 in 184 ÷ 8). Ask: "Does 8 fit in 1?" Nothing goes there.
  • Bringing down two digits at once after a too-small step (in 618 ÷ 6, jumping from 1 straight to 18). Point to the steps: one digit at a time, and every bring-down is followed by a divide.
  • Digits drifting out of their columns on paper. Have her align each product under the number it came from; graph paper or lined paper turned sideways helps.
  • Subtraction slips inside the steps (writing 1 instead of 2 for 25 − 23). Have her say each subtraction out loud.
  • "I can't do it" when she sees 3 digits. Cover all but the first digit with a card: "Just this one. Does 6 fit in 6?" Praise, then uncover the next digit.
