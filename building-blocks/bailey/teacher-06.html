Teacher Plan · Lesson 6: Stop Adding, Start Multiplying

Monday, November 2, 2026 · Zoom · 60 minutes · Week 3: The Area Model: Stop Adding, Start Multiplying · Focus: Area model / choosing the efficient method

🎯 Objective: Bailey multiplies a 2-digit number by a 1-digit number with a two-box area model (75 × 8 = 70 × 8 + 5 × 8) and can explain why it beats adding 8 seventy-five times. This is the direct fix for her habit of adding a number over and over.
Student goal: I can use an area model to multiply a 2-digit number by a 1-digit number.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 90

Materials

  • Zoom with screen share of the lesson
  • Her own whiteboard or paper and a marker (she holds work up to the camera)
  • Grid paper and 2 colored pencils for drawing rectangles
  • Her book, How to Train Your Dragon
  • Printed packet (2 pages), sent to Tia before the lesson

60-minute plan

0:00–0:07Book Talk
Slide 2. She retells the last part in order. Push on prompt 2: Hiccup wins with brains, not muscle. Tie it to today: we stop using math muscle (adding) and use math brains (multiplying).
0:07–0:15Word Power
Slide 3. She flips all 8 cards and reads each example aloud. Have her use laborious and immense in her own sentence before moving on.
0:15–0:23Why adding is slow + facts with a zero
Slides 4–6. Ask her to start adding 8 + 8 + 8 … on her whiteboard for 30 seconds, then stop: how many did she get through? Then show 70 × 8 as 7 tens × 8 = 56 tens. She says each tens fact aloud before tapping.
0:23–0:36Area model
Slides 7–9. She draws the 75 × 8 rectangle on grid paper with one color for the 70 box and one for the 5 box, and holds it up. Then 46 × 7 on paper before tapping. On the order slide she must say each step aloud.
0:36–0:43Problems + recall
Slides 10–11. She draws the area model on her whiteboard for each word problem before choosing. If a fact stalls, send her back to the shortcut (×9 = ×10 minus one group, ×8 = double, double, double).
0:43–0:48Word Power check-in
Slide 12. She explains why each wrong sentence is wrong before moving on.
0:48–0:56Show What You Know
Slide 13 on her own, camera on, paper allowed. One try each. She needs 90 (9 of 10) to open Lesson 7.
0:56–1:00Wrap-up
Share the course page task list. Tomorrow is an in-person lesson, so tonight is short: the practice set and the packet.

Watch for

  • Dropping the zero (70 × 8 = 56). Ask: is 70 a ones number or a tens number? Say it as 7 tens × 8 = 56 tens.
  • Adding the factors (75 + 8 = 83) when she rushes. Ask: are there 8 equal groups of 75? Then multiply.
  • Multiplying only the tens box and writing the ones digit (560 + 5 = 565). Point to the ones box: it is a rectangle too, so it gets multiplied.
  • Missing regrouping when adding partial products (280 + 42 = 312). Have her add tens to tens out loud.
  • "I can't do it." Point to the rectangle: it is only two facts she already knows. Have her do one box at a time.
