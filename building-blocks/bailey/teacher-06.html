Teacher Plan · Lesson 6: Stop Adding, Start Multiplying

Monday, November 2, 2026 · Zoom · 60 minutes · Week 3: The Area Model: Stop Adding, Start Multiplying · Focus: Area model / choosing the efficient method

🎯 Objective: Bailey multiplies a 2-digit number by a 1-digit number with a two-box area model (75 × 8 = 70 × 8 + 5 × 8) and can explain why it beats adding 8 seventy-five times. This is the direct fix for her habit of adding a number over and over.
Student goal: I can use an area model to multiply a 2-digit number by a 1-digit number.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 90

Materials

  • Zoom with screen share of the Class Lesson (teacher code 9110)
  • At home: her whiteboard (or paper) and a marker to hold up to the camera
  • At home: 3 cups and about 50 dry beans (for the 'Why splitting works' slide)
  • At home: grid paper and 2 colored pencils
  • Her book, How to Train Your Dragon
  • Packet (2 pages): assigned on Tuesday's task list, so it does not need to be printed today

60-minute plan

0:00–0:02Hello + materials check
Class slide 1. She holds up each item to the camera. Ask for her guess: is 75 × 8 closer to 60, 600 or 6,000?
0:02–0:08Book Talk
Class slide 2. She retells in order. Push on prompt 2: Hiccup wins with brains, not muscle. Tie it to today: math brains (multiplying) beat math muscle (adding).
0:08–0:14Word Power
Class slide 3. She tells you which card to flip, reads each aloud and makes her own sentence with laborious, immense and abandon.
0:14–0:18Why adding is slow (30-second race)
Class slide 4. 30-second timer: she writes 8 + 8 + 8 … with a running total. Stop and discuss how far 75 eights would take.
0:18–0:24Facts with a zero
Class slides 5–6. 7 tens × 8 = 56 tens = 560 on her whiteboard. She says each tens fact aloud before telling you her choice.
0:24–0:29Hands-on: beans in cups
Class slide 7. 3 cups of 14 beans; she splits each cup into 10 and 4 and counts 30 + 12 = 42.
0:29–0:35Meet the area model
Class slide 8. She draws 75 × 8 on grid paper (two colors), writes 560 and 40 inside, adds to 600 and compares to her guess.
0:35–0:41Fill the boxes + order the steps
Class slides 9–10. 46 × 7 on paper before she picks an answer; she says each step of 57 × 8 aloud.
0:41–0:45Guided practice: she leads
Class slide 11. She teaches you 38 × 9 box by box, whiteboard up after each box.
0:45–0:49Problems on Berk
Class slide 12. She draws the area model for each story before she picks an answer.
0:49–0:52Fact shortcut recall
Class slide 13. She names the shortcut (×9, ×8, ×6) before she picks an answer.
0:52–0:55Word Power check-in
Class slide 14. She explains why each wrong choice is wrong.
0:55–0:58Exit ticket
Class slide 15 on her own: 63 × 7 and the partial-product word. No hints.
0:58–1:00Wrap-up + tonight's tasks
Class slide 16. She says the three steps from memory. Show the course page: tonight is only the After-Class Lesson (ends with Show What You Know) and 10 minutes of reading.

Watch for

  • Dropping the zero (70 × 8 = 56). Ask: is 70 a ones number or a tens number? Say it as 7 tens × 8 = 56 tens.
  • Adding the factors (75 + 8 = 83) when she rushes. Ask: are there 8 equal groups of 75? Then multiply.
  • Multiplying only the tens box and writing the ones digit (560 + 5 = 565). Point to the ones box: it is a rectangle too, so it gets multiplied.
  • Missing regrouping when adding partial products (280 + 42 = 312). Have her add tens to tens out loud.
  • "I can't do it." Point to the rectangle: it is only two facts she already knows. Have her do one box at a time.
