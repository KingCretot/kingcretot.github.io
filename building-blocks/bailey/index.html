Smart Shortcuts for ×9 and ×6

🎯 I can use ×10 and ×5 facts to find ×9 and ×6 facts fast.

Bailey stops adding the same number over and over. In the class lesson Jonathan teaches two shortcuts built on facts she already knows: ×9 = ×10 minus one group (9 × 7 = 70 − 7) and ×6 = ×5 plus one group (6 × 8 = 40 + 8), plus the 9s digit checks to verify her work. The after-class lesson has her type the facts and the vocabulary from memory. Vocabulary: factor, product, strategy, efficient, tedious, ingenious, verify.

Break It Apart: The 7s

🎯 I can break 7 into 5 + 2 to find any ×7 fact.

Bailey learns to break apart a hard fact: 7 × 6 = (5 × 6) + (2 × 6) = 30 + 12 = 42. She splits real counter arrays with a pencil, learns the 7 × 8 = 56 "5, 6, 7, 8" trick, and spots broken break-aparts. This is the idea behind the area model in Week 3. New words: decompose, addend, distributive, fragment, reassemble, cunning, tactic.

Division Undoes Multiplication

🎯 I can solve a division fact by asking the multiplication question, like 56 ÷ 8 means 8 × ? = 56.

Bailey learns that division is multiplication run backward: 56 ÷ 8 means 8 × ? = 56. In the Zoom class lesson she shares beans both ways (how many in each, how many groups), makes fact triangles for the 6–9 facts, and learns dividend, divisor, quotient and four more words; the after-class lesson has her do it on her own.
