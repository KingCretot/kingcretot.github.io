Smart Shortcuts for ×9 and ×6

🎯 I can use ×10 and ×5 facts to find ×9 and ×6 facts fast.

Bailey stops adding the same number over and over. She learns two shortcuts built on facts she already knows: ×9 = ×10 minus one group (9 × 7 = 70 − 7) and ×6 = ×5 plus one group (6 × 8 = 40 + 8), plus the 9s digit patterns to check her work. Vocabulary: factor, product, strategy, efficient, tedious, ingenious, verify.

Double, Double, Double: The 8s

🎯 I can find any ×8 fact by doubling three times.

Bailey learns that ×4 is double, double and ×8 is double, double, double (8 × 7: 14, 28, 56). Three quick doubles replace adding 8 over and over, which is where she loses track. New words: double, halve, quadruple, hoard, gargantuan, formidable, amplify.

Break It Apart: The 7s

🎯 I can break 7 into 5 + 2 to find any ×7 fact.

Bailey learns to break apart a hard fact: 7 × 6 = (5 × 6) + (2 × 6) = 30 + 12 = 42. She splits real counter arrays with a pencil, learns the 7 × 8 = 56 "5, 6, 7, 8" trick, and spots broken break-aparts. This is the idea behind the area model in Week 3. New words: decompose, addend, distributive, fragment, reassemble, cunning, tactic.

Stop Adding, Start Multiplying

🎯 I can use an area model to multiply a 2-digit number by a 1-digit number.

Bailey learns the area model: split 75 into 70 + 5, multiply each part by 8, then add (560 + 40 = 600). It replaces her habit of adding 8 over and over, and it runs on the fact shortcuts from Weeks 1 and 2.

Division Undoes Multiplication

🎯 I can solve a division fact by asking the multiplication question, like 56 ÷ 8 means 8 × ? = 56.

Bailey learns that division is multiplication run backward: 56 ÷ 8 means 8 × ? = 56. She meets the two kinds of division question (how many in each, how many groups), reads fact triangles for the 6–9 facts, and learns dividend, divisor, quotient and four more words.

🎯 I can write all the facts in a fact family and tell what is left over when things don't share evenly.

Bailey turns each fact triangle into a full fact family (two multiplication facts, two division facts) and learns which facts do not belong. Then she meets remainders with small numbers like 30 ÷ 7, using beans and cups, plus seven new words.
