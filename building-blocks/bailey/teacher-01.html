Teacher Plan · Lesson 1: Smart Shortcuts for ×9 and ×6

Tuesday, October 20, 2026 · In person · 60 minutes · Week 1: Fact Shortcuts: x9 and x6 · Focus: Facts 6–9 automatic + fact strategies

🎯 Objective: Bailey replaces repeated adding with two fact strategies built on facts she already knows: ×9 = ×10 minus one group, and ×6 = ×5 plus one group. She uses the 9s digit-sum and tens-digit patterns to verify answers, and learns 7 vocabulary words (factor, product, strategy, efficient, tedious, ingenious, verify).
Student goal: I can use ×10 and ×5 facts to find ×9 and ×6 facts fast.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 90

Materials

  • About 80 counters or dried beans
  • Whiteboard and marker
  • 7 index cards, one vocabulary word on each
  • Her book, How to Train Your Dragon
  • Printed packet (2 pages) to send home

60-minute plan

0:00–0:07Book Talk
Slide 2. She retells the last part she read in order. Push on conflict: what is Hiccup's problem, and how does he use his brain instead of muscles? This sets up today's theme: work smart, not long.
0:07–0:15Word Power
Slide 3. She flips each card, reads it, and says her own sentence. Write each word on an index card and keep the cards on the table all lesson. Every time she uses a word correctly, hand her the card.
0:15–0:21Why adding is slow
Slide 4. Race: she adds 7 nine times on the whiteboard while you say "70 minus 7 is 63." Ask: Which was more efficient? Which was more tedious? Point out how one slip ruins every number after it.
0:21–0:30×9 shortcut (hands-on)
Before slide 5, she builds 10 rows of 7 counters. Ask: how many? (70). Slide away one row and ask: how many rows now? how many counters? Then slides 5–6. She must say the subtraction out loud before tapping.
0:30–0:37Verify 9s
Slides 7–8. Teach the finger check with her hands. In the sort, make her say which check she used. The 9 × 7 = 54 card is the tricky one: it passes the digit check but fails the tens-digit check.
0:37–0:46×6 shortcut
Slides 9–11. Rebuild with counters: 5 rows of 8, then add one more row. In the build, she says 35 + 7 before she taps Check.
0:46–0:50Think smart
Slide 12. She reads each problem aloud and names the strategy before choosing.
0:50–0:57Show What You Know
Slide 13, on her own. One try each. She needs 90 (9 of 10) to open Lesson 2. Do not help; note which ones she hesitates on.
0:57–1:00Wrap-up
Open the course page and read the task list with her. Packet page 1 Wednesday, page 2 before Thursday. She keeps the vocab index cards to review at home and brings them back Thursday.

Watch for

  • Falling back to repeated adding (her "add it 75 times" habit). Say: "Stop. What do you already know? 10 × 7." Then wait.
  • Taking away 9 instead of the other factor (9 × 7 = 70 − 9 = 61). Ask: "How many are in ONE group?" Point to one row of counters.
  • Adding 6 instead of the other factor for ×6 (6 × 8 = 40 + 6 = 46). Same fix: "What is in one row?"
  • Mixing the two shortcuts (adding for ×9, subtracting for ×6). Ask: "Is 9 more or less than 10? Is 6 more or less than 5?"
  • "I can't do it." Reframe: "You already know 10s and 5s. This is just one more step." Have her do the counters, then return to the slide.
