Teacher Plan · Lesson 17: What Do I Do with the Remainder?

Tuesday, December 15, 2026 · In person · 60 minutes · Week 7: Remainders and Checking · Focus: Long division, remainders + checking

🎯 Objective: Bailey interprets remainders in word problems: she solves with long division, rereads the question, and decides whether to round up (everyone or everything must fit), drop the remainder (only full groups count), or give the remainder as the answer (what is left over), then writes a practical answer with a label.
Student goal: I can decide what a remainder means in a story: round up, drop it, or make it the answer.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 90

Materials

  • Class lesson on your laptop or tablet (teacher code 9110)
  • 50 dried beans and 8 cups (the boats)
  • 3 sticky notes: Round up, Drop it, Remainder is the answer
  • Whiteboard and marker
  • Paper and a pencil for Bailey
  • Her book, How to Train Your Dragon
  • Packet (2 pages): page 1 on Wednesday, page 2 over the break

Watch for

  • Writing "6 R 2 boats" as the answer. Ask: "Can you rent part of a boat? What is practical?"
  • Rounding up every time (or never). Ask: "Read the question again. Does everyone need to fit, or only full ones?"
  • Giving the quotient when the question asks what is left over. Ask: "What does Gobber keep?"
  • Deciding before solving and then forgetting the remainder. Keep the order: solve, reread, decide.
  • Long division slips in the 3-digit stories (130 ÷ 8: 8 does not fit in 1, start with 13). Praise each correct step.
  • "I can't do it" on a long story. Have her read it aloud and ask only: "What is the total, and what is the size of each group?"
