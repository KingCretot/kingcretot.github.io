Teacher Plan · Lesson 16: Leftovers in Long Division

Monday, December 14, 2026 · Zoom · 60 minutes · Week 7: Remainders and Checking · Focus: Long division, remainders + checking

🎯 Objective: Bailey does long division with remainders for 2- and 3-digit dividends ÷ 1-digit divisors (97 ÷ 4 = 24 R 1, 853 ÷ 6 = 142 R 1). She knows the remainder must be smaller than the divisor (the maximum remainder is the divisor minus 1), revises an answer whose remainder is too big, and checks every answer with quotient × divisor + remainder = dividend.
Student goal: I can do long division with a remainder, make sure the remainder is smaller than the divisor, and check my answer.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 90

Watch for

  • A remainder as big as or bigger than the divisor (83 ÷ 9 = 8 R 11). Ask: "Could one more group of 9 fit?"
  • Forgetting to add the remainder when checking (24 × 4 = 96 and stopping). Point to the whiteboard rule: multiply, THEN add.
  • Writing the remainder as a digit in the quotient (97 ÷ 4 = 241). Ask: "Is there a digit up top for the 1 to sit over?" It goes after R.
  • A leftover in the middle step that is too big (in 853 ÷ 6, writing 3 for 25 ÷ 6 and leaving 7). Have her say "Is it smaller than 6?" after every subtraction.
  • Dropping a placeholder 0 when there is also a remainder (617 ÷ 3 = 25 R 2). Ask: "Estimate first: about 20 or about 200?"
  • "I can't do it." Ask only: "What is the first step?" When she does it, praise that one step out loud, then ask for the next.
