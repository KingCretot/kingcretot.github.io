Teacher Plan · Lesson 10: Fact Families and Leftovers

Tuesday, November 10, 2026 · In person · 60 minutes · Week 4: Division Undoes Multiplication · Focus: Division facts and fact families

🎯 Objective: Bailey writes all four facts in a fact family for the 6–9 facts (and knows a square like 7, 7, 49 has only two), spots facts that do not belong, and divides with a small leftover: finds the biggest fact that fits, subtracts, and writes the answer as a remainder (30 ÷ 7 = 4 R 2), knowing the remainder must be smaller than the divisor.
Student goal: I can write all the facts in a fact family and tell what is left over when things don't share evenly.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 90

Materials

  • Class lesson on your laptop or tablet (teacher code 9110)
  • About 50 dried beans or counters
  • 7 small cups or paper plates
  • Whiteboard and marker
  • Index cards: her three triangle cards from Lesson 9 plus 5 blank cards
  • Her book, How to Train Your Dragon
  • Packet (2 pages): page 1 due Thursday, page 2 over the break

0:19–0:27Fact families (hands-on)
Class slides 5–6. Four index cards for 6, 8, 48; she discovers 7, 7, 49 has only two facts.
0:27–0:33Who belongs?
Class slides 7–8. Pick the family member; decide in or out for the 6, 9, 54 family and say why.
0:33–0:42Leftovers with beans
Class slides 9–11. Deal 30 beans into 7 cups; fix Fishlegs' 38 ÷ 6 = 5 R 8 with beans; predict then build 26 ÷ 6.
0:42–0:48Guided leftovers and steps
Class slides 12–13. Which fact fits, how many left, is the remainder smaller than the divisor; say the five steps before tapping.
0:48–0:54Word work and exit ticket
Class slides 14–15. Clue words and one sentence with surplus and remainder; then three exit-ticket items on her own. Note any miss for Thursday.
0:54–1:00Wrap-up
Class slide 16. Walk through the two-night task list on the course page: tonight the After-class lesson and Lesson 10 Practice; Wednesday the Lesson 9 Practice, Lesson 9 Packet and Lesson 10 Packet page 1; reading both nights.

Watch for

  • Writing backward division facts (6 ÷ 54 = 9) in a family. Say: the top of the triangle comes first in division.
  • Counting 8 × 9 and 9 × 8 as two different families, or writing 4 facts for 7, 7, 49. Have her write them and see the repeats.
  • Going over: choosing 7 × 5 = 35 for 30 ÷ 7. Ask: did you use more fish than you have?
  • A remainder as big as the divisor (38 ÷ 6 = 5 R 8). Ask: could every dragon get one more?
  • Forgetting the remainder and writing just the quotient. Ask: where did the leftover fish go?
  • Shutting down on a bigger number. Ask: which fact do you know that is close? Then wait for her.
