Teacher Plan · Lesson 2: Prove It: Every ×9 and ×6 Fact

Thursday, October 22, 2026 · In person · 60 minutes · Week 1: Fact Shortcuts: x9 and x6 · Focus: Facts 6–9 automatic + fact strategies

🎯 Objective: Bailey shows she owns every ×6 and ×9 fact to 10: she uses the turn-around (commutative) idea, finds missing factors (9 × ? = 63), spots and fixes flawed facts, and solves one- and two-step word problems. She learns 7 vocabulary words (multiple, equation, commutative, justify, flawed, reluctant, persevere) and reviews Tuesday's words.
Student goal: I can solve any ×9 or ×6 fact, find a missing factor, and fix a wrong fact.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 90

Materials

  • About 60 counters
  • A sheet of paper to rotate as an array (6 rows of 9)
  • Whiteboard and marker
  • Tuesday's 7 vocab index cards plus 7 new cards
  • Her book, How to Train Your Dragon
  • Printed packet (2 pages) to send home

60-minute plan

0:00–0:07Book Talk
Slide 2. She retells what she read since Tuesday. Push her to use ingenious or strategy in a full sentence. Ask how Hiccup has changed.
0:07–0:12Recall
Slide 3. Tuesday's shortcuts and one vocab word. If she misses the ×9 or ×6 item, rebuild it with counters right away (10 rows minus one; 5 rows plus one).
0:12–0:19Word Power
Slide 4. She flips, reads and makes a sentence for each word. Ask: When have YOU been reluctant? When did you persevere? Add the 7 new index cards to Tuesday's pile.
0:19–0:24All the facts
Slide 5. Quick-fire: you say a ×6 or ×9 fact, she answers and names the shortcut. Point out the even pattern for 6s.
0:24–0:30Turn-around (hands-on)
Slide 6. Lay out 6 rows of 9 counters on a sheet of paper. Turn the paper sideways: now it is 9 rows of 6. Ask: did any counters change? Then say 7 × 9 and 8 × 6 and have her flip them.
0:30–0:37Missing factors
Slides 7–8. She must say the equation with the word "times what" ("9 times what is 63?") and verify every answer by multiplying back.
0:37–0:44True or flawed
Slides 9–10. Before each tap she says which check or shortcut she used. On slide 10, ask her to explain the mistake in her own words, not just pick the fix.
0:44–0:50Word problems
Slides 11–12. In the order slide, have her say each step aloud. On the fish problem, cover the answer choices and let her solve it on the whiteboard first.
0:50–0:57Show What You Know
Slide 13, on her own. One try each. She needs 90 (9 of 10) to open Lesson 3.
0:57–1:00Wrap-up
Show the task list and the Times Table Trail Lesson 4 link on the course page. Monday's lesson is on Zoom: she holds up her packet to show you.

Watch for

  • Answering missing-factor problems with the total or a random fact (9 × ? = 63 → 54). Ask: "Say it as a question: 9 times what is 63?" Then make her verify.
  • Not flipping a fact she doesn't know (7 × 9). Ask: "Which factor has a shortcut?" and point to the 9.
  • Two-step word problems: she stops after the multiplication (54 fish) and forgets the second step. Ask: "Did anything else happen in the story?"
  • Using only the digit-sum check (accepting 9 × 3 = 72). Remind her: two checks catch more.
  • Choosing the slowest method or saying "I can't." Say: "You already proved you know 10s and 5s. Which shortcut fits?" Praise perseverance by name: "That's persevering."
