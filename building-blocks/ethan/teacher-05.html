Teacher Plan · Lesson 5: Feast Report and the Unit 2 Checkpoint

Wednesday, December 2, 2026 · In person · 60 minutes · Week 10: Unit 2 Checkpoint · Focus: Review of Weeks 6–9

🎯 Objective: First lesson back after the two-week break. Ethan presents his Feast Report poster from the Ferguson Family Feast Planner (budget, scaled recipe, leftovers, two viewpoints, taste-test) and connects each part to a Unit 2 skill. Math review (27 min): the standard algorithm with an estimate (Week 6), long division with 2-digit divisors, remainders and the inverse-operation check (Week 7), adding, subtracting and comparing fractions with unlike denominators in simplest form (Week 8), multiplying decimals, fraction-decimal pairs and half a pound (Week 9), and two-skill problems: a fraction of a number after adding fractions, and money totals and change. Reading review (15 min): character change and turning point, comparing two viewpoints, author's purpose, text structure from signal words, and a conclusion that uses two sources. He learns 7 new words (simplest form, multi-step problem, inverse operations, character change, turning point, gradual, inference) and leaves with a plan for the Pathways Unit 2 Checkpoint on Monday.

Watch for

  • Presenting: let him finish before you ask anything. If his budget math has a slip (decimal points not lined up, a wrong subtraction), redo it together on the whiteboard as a warm-up, not as a correction. This is his project and his win.
  • The missing 0 (Week 6): the tens row of 53 × 27 is 1,060, not 106. If he forgets it, his answer will be far below the estimate. Make him say “write the 0” out loud.
  • Remainders (Week 7): the remainder must be smaller than the divisor (100 ÷ 16 is 6 R4, not 5 R20). In stories, round UP when everyone must fit (buses, boxes). The Pathways Checkpoint's Week 7 division has no remainder in the typed question, but its multiple-choice division does.
  • Simplest form (Week 8): the Checkpoint accepts 1/2 or 3/6 on its typed fraction question, but our questions and most of Pathways want simplest form. Watch for the bigger-denominator trap when comparing.
  • Decimal point (Week 9): count the decimal places and check with an estimate. Money needs two decimal places ($20.10, not $20.1). Half a pound = × 0.5 = half the price; watch for him doubling instead.
  • Two-skill questions: the Checkpoint's Mixed and Boss questions are worth about 30% of the score, and most are multi-step money or fraction problems (a fraction of a number, a total with half a pound). Underline what the question asks for: used or left, total or change.
  • Reading: best vs. true. On the Checkpoint several choices can sound true. Push for the BEST: the main concern, the strongest evidence, the conclusion that uses both sources and admits a downside.
  • Typing on the Checkpoint: it gives only 2 tries per question, and only a first-try answer earns points. It ignores commas, spaces and $, so 1431, 1,431 and $8.50 or 8.50 all work. He types fractions with a slash (1/2). He has lost points on typed answers that were right on paper, so he compares the screen to his paper before pressing Check.
  • Curly apostrophes on the iPad: not needed on the Checkpoint (no typed words), but on our after-class lesson he may type words; turn off Smart Punctuation if a typed word is marked wrong.
