Teacher Plan · Lesson 1: Malik's Mural: Multiplying Big Numbers

Wednesday, October 21, 2026 · In person · 60 minutes · Week 6: Malik's Mural Project · Focus: Multi-digit multiplication: area model, partial products, standard algorithm

🎯 Objective: Ethan multiplies 2-digit × 2-digit and 3-digit × 2-digit numbers accurately and on his own: estimate first (round to the nearest ten), set up the partial products from the area model (18 × 13 = 180 + 54 = 234), line up place value with the 0 in the tens row, regroup correctly, add the rows, and check the answer against the estimate. He also spots a wrong step in someone else's work and types the math words (factor, product, partial product, estimate, regroup) from memory.
Student goal: I can estimate first, then multiply 2-digit and 3-digit numbers by 2-digit numbers step by step and check my answer.

Watch for

  • Regrouping: watch his pencil right after the ones digit. The regrouped number must be written small above the next digit and added AFTER multiplying (8 × 2 = 16, then 16 + 3 = 19). If he adds first (2 + 3 = 5, then × 8) or drops it, say: multiply, then add the little number.
  • Old regroup marks: before the tens row he crosses out the regrouped numbers from the ones row, or he will add them twice.
  • The 0 in the second partial product: he writes the 0 in the ones place BEFORE multiplying by the tens digit. Writing 54 + 18 = 72 for 18 × 13 means the 0 is missing (the second row must be 180); ask, What is the 1 in 13 really worth?
  • Lining up columns: ones under ones, especially when one row has 4 digits (136 × 12, 214 × 23). Use lined paper turned sideways.
  • Adding the rows: he adds the partial products one column at a time and regroups in the addition too (141 + 940 = 1,081, not 1,181). The estimate does NOT catch these small adding slips, so he must recheck.
  • Typing the final answer: he copies the number from his paper exactly and checks it before pressing Check. Commas are fine either way. A wrong digit typed is a lost point even when the paper is right.
  • Rushing: if he answers in under 20 seconds on a 2-digit × 2-digit problem, or skips the estimate, stop him: say the estimate first, then go. Slow and right is the goal today.
  • Word problems: he missed 18 × 13 inside a word problem before but could do the bare problem. Have him write the number sentence (36 × 12) before he solves.
