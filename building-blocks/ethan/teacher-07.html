Teacher Plan · Lesson 7: The Houston Apartment Build and Building Up Houston

Wednesday, January 6, 2027 · In person · 60 minutes · Week 12: The Houston Apartment Build · Focus: Equivalent fractions, simplifying and mixed numbers + informative writing: paragraphs and transitions (Pathways Week 12)

🎯 Objective: Math (35 min): Ethan shows with tile models that equivalent fractions name the same amount (3/8 = 6/16 = 9/24), finds a missing numerator or denominator by multiplying or dividing by n/n (and explains why adding to the top and bottom does not work), simplifies a fraction in one step by dividing by the greatest common factor from Lesson 6 and checks that the result is in simplest form, converts improper fractions to mixed numbers by dividing (the remainder becomes the numerator over the same denominator) and mixed numbers to improper fractions (whole × denominator + numerator), and solves Pathways-style two-step problems: the fraction of tiles that are the OTHER color in simplest form, and a line of unit-fraction pipes written as a mixed number in simplest form. Reading (25 min): he names the parts of an informative paragraph (topic sentence, supporting details, transitions, concluding sentence), matches transitions to their jobs (order, example, contrast, effect, add) with the comma rule, finds the topic sentence and the off-topic sentence in a two-paragraph article, puts a jumbled paragraph in order, and types the reading words from memory.
Student goal: I can find equivalent fractions, simplify a fraction in one step with the GCF, convert between improper fractions and mixed numbers, and build an informative paragraph with a topic sentence, details, transitions and a concluding sentence.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 85

Watch for

  • Adding instead of multiplying: 3/8 → 4/9 or 5/6 = 30/31. Go back to the hallway strips: cutting every tile in 2 doubles BOTH numbers. Adding to both numbers always changes the amount.
  • Stopping before simplest form: 36/54 ÷ 9 = 4/6, or 20/36 → 10/18. He uses the GCF from his factor lists (Lesson 6) and then asks, “Do the new numbers share any factor except 1?” Pathways marks 6/8 wrong when it asks for simplest form.
  • Converting: the remainder goes over the SAME denominator (19/4 = 4 3/4, not 4 3/19), and for mixed → improper he multiplies the whole number by the DENOMINATOR (5 2/3 = 17/3, not 10/3). Checking backward catches both.
  • Typing mixed numbers: a space between the whole number and the fraction (4 3/4). Our lessons also accept 4-3/4 and 4 and 3/4; Pathways keeps the space and reads 43/4 as a different number. After converting, he simplifies the fraction part (3 2/8 → 3 1/4).
  • Two-step tile questions: they ask for the OTHER color (white, not blue). He underlines the color in the question before he subtracts. Forgetting step 1 gives the blue fraction.
  • Pathways Week 12 Math typed answers: missing-numerator questions accept the number or the full fraction (12 or 12/20); simplify questions need simplest form; it gives only 2 tries and only the first try earns points. Its math lesson also has a Fix the Sentence round (quotation marks from Lesson 6's dialogue, its vs. it's, commas in a series, subject-verb agreement): he retypes the sentence with the same words in the same order and fixes only capitals, punctuation or the one grammar mistake.
  • Transition jobs: however is a contrast, not “one more detail” (use also); as a result needs a real cause; for example needs a real example of the sentence before it. A comma goes right after a transition that starts a sentence.
  • Pathways Week 12 Reading: it reads a full article about the Houston build and asks for topic sentences, main ideas, the conclusion, which paragraph a new sentence belongs in, the off-topic sentence, and the best transition. Its Fix the Sentence round wants the exact sentence retyped (comma after the transition, capitals for names like Houston and Gulf of Mexico, verbs that agree, there/their, apostrophes). The Writer's Notebook paragraph earns XP only. Its Boss questions put four sentences in order using the topic sentence and transitions, which he practiced in class with different paragraphs.
