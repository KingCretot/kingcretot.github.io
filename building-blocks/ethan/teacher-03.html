Teacher Plan · Lesson 3: Sophia's Garden Plot and Ravi's Newsletter

Wednesday, November 4, 2026 · In person · 60 minutes · Week 8: Sophia's Garden Plot · Focus: Fractions with unlike denominators (Pathways Week 8)

🎯 Objective: Math (35 min): Ethan finds a common denominator for fractions with unlike denominators, renames fractions as equivalent fractions (multiplying the numerator and the denominator by the same number), adds and subtracts them, writes every answer in simplest form, writes a sum greater than 1 whole as a mixed number, compares unlike fractions, and checks with an estimate using 1/2 and 1. Reading (25 min): he identifies the author's purpose (inform, persuade, entertain) of each piece in Ravi's Newsletter, tells purpose from point of view, finds evidence in the crosswalk letter, explains how the bakery spotlight blends two purposes, sorts pieces into News, Opinions and Fun & Games, and types the math and reading words from memory.
Student goal: I can add and subtract fractions with unlike denominators, write my answer in simplest form, and tell why an author wrote a piece.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 85

Watch for

  • Adding the denominators: writing 2/7 as the answer to 1/3 + 1/4 is the big one. Catch it with an estimate: the answer must be bigger than each part, and 2/7 is less than 1/3.
  • Renaming only the bottom: 1/2 + 1/8 written as 1/8 + 1/8. Whatever he multiplies the denominator by, he multiplies the numerator by too. On renaming items, watch for him typing the multiplier (4) instead of the new numerator (8).
  • Simplest form: Pathways Week 8 marks most answers right ONLY in simplest form (it wants 5/6, not 10/12, and 3/4, not 6/8). It accepts 3/12 or 1/4 and 9/12 or 3/4 on the two crisis questions only. Using the SMALLEST common denominator saves a simplifying step. He checks: can the numerator and denominator both be divided by the same number?
  • More than 1 whole: if the numerator is bigger than the denominator, the answer is more than 1 whole. Our questions say when they want a mixed number (type it like 1 1/2). Pathways Week 8 itself has no answers over 1, but its paper challenge (design your own plot with three fractions) can go over 1.
  • The bigger-denominator trap: “6 is bigger than 4, so 1/6 is bigger.” Pathways has this exact wrong choice. More pieces means smaller pieces; prove it with the strips or with twelfths (3/12 vs 2/12).
  • Typing fractions: he types 5/6 with a slash, no spaces needed. He has lost points on typed answers that were right on paper, so he compares the typed answer to his paper before pressing Check.
  • Purpose vs point of view: purpose is WHY (inform, persuade, entertain); point of view is WHAT the writer believes. The bakery spotlight both informs and persuades; its MAIN purpose is persuade.
  • Pathways Week 8 Reading typed answers: it accepts inform, persuade, evidence and author's purpose as typed. On the Boss question “in a few words, what is this whole lesson really about?” it only accepts one exact word or phrase: purpose, inform, entertain or point of view (on the first question set it does NOT accept “persuade” or “author's purpose”). Tell him to type purpose there.
  • Curly apostrophe on the iPad: Pathways Week 8 Reading only accepts “author's purpose” typed with a straight apostrophe ('). iPad autocorrect turns it into a curly one (’) and the answer is marked wrong. Have him type just purpose where that works, or turn off Smart Punctuation (Settings, General, Keyboard) before he starts.
