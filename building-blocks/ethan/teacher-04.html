Teacher Plan · Lesson 4: Elena's Market Stand and The Sleep Study

Wednesday, December 2, 2026 · In person · 60 minutes · Week 9: Elena's Market Stand · Focus: Multiplying decimals + lining up decimal points (Pathways Week 9)

🎯 Objective: Math (35 min): Ethan reads tenths and hundredths (with money), converts the key fractions to decimals (1/2, 1/4, 3/4, 1/10, 1/5, 2/5), multiplies a decimal by a whole number and a decimal by a decimal by multiplying without the points and counting the decimal places, uses × 0.5 for half a pound, checks that each answer is reasonable with an estimate, and lines up decimal points to total an order and make change from $10 and $20 (writing $20 as 20.00 and regrouping across zeros). Reading (25 min): he names the text structure of a nonfiction piece (cause and effect, problem and solution, sequence, compare and contrast) from its signal words and its overall job, reads both Pathways Week 9 sources (Why Teens Are So Tired; One District's Answer), synthesizes them into one sentence that names each source and admits the downside, and types the math and reading words from memory.
Student goal: I can multiply decimals and put the decimal point in the right place, line up decimal points to total an order and make change, and tell how a nonfiction text is built.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 85

Watch for

  • The point in the wrong place: 2.4 × 0.3 written as 7.2 or 72. He counts the decimal places in BOTH factors and adds them, then checks with an estimate: multiplying by a number less than 1 makes the answer smaller.
  • Lining up the points when multiplying: he does not need to. Multiply: line up on the right and count places. Add or subtract: line up the points. Pathways Week 9's “Ignore the decimal, count the places, put it back” is the same method.
  • Lining up the last digits when adding: 3.5 + 1.25 written as 1.60, or 4.75 + 2.6 as 5.01. Grid paper and filling in zeros (2.6 → 2.60) fix it.
  • Change from $20: writing 20 as 20.00 and regrouping across the zeros; the slip is $11.45 instead of $11.35 for $20 − $8.65. Counting up (to the next dollar, then to $20) is the safer method, and adding back checks it.
  • More digits = bigger: “0.45 is more than 0.5.” Write both with the same number of places (0.45 vs 0.50), or use coins.
  • Pathways Week 9 Math typed answers: it removes commas, $ and spaces and reads the number, so 4.8 and 4.80, $7.20 and 7.2, and 0.5 and .5 are all accepted. A misplaced decimal point (72 or 0.72 for 7.2) is marked wrong. Word answers (hundredths, tenths, estimate) need the exact spelling. Pathways gives only 2 tries per question (our lessons give 3).
  • Text structure vs author's purpose: purpose (Lesson 3) is WHY the author wrote it; structure is HOW the information is organized. One signal word is not enough (“so” and “because” show up everywhere); he asks what the WHOLE piece does.
  • Pathways Week 9 Reading typed answers: it accepts any answer that contains the key word (structure, synthes, source, evidence), so typing text structure or synthesize on the Boss question works, and problem and solution works on set 3. Its multiple-choice questions ask about Source A's and Source B's structure, signal words, the downside and the strongest synthesized conclusion; he practiced all of these ideas in class with different questions.
