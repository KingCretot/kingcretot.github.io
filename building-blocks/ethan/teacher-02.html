Watch for

  • Tens row: he must say the tens digit as tens (“forty”) and write the 0 first. For 56 × 40 he wrote 324 (should be 2,240): he dropped the 0, and his 56 × 4 step also slipped (it is 224).
  • Columns and adding: one digit per square on graph paper; regrouped 1s written above the column. 1,786 for 68 × 27 (1,836) and 2,848 for 76 × 38 (2,888) were adding errors; 1,350 for 135 × 12 (1,620) was only the tens row: he left out the ones row (270).
  • Estimate: 70 × 20 = 1,400, not 14,000. He writes the estimate in a box before every problem.
  • Rushing on retakes: his scores have dropped on repeat tries (Pathways Week 6 Math 80, 63, 55; Week 3 Reading 75, 25). Retakes come after the problems are worked on paper, one retake a day.
  • Where the first digit goes: when the divisor does not fit into the first digit (552 ÷ 6), the first quotient digit goes over the SECOND digit. If he writes 0 or a digit over the 5, ask: how many 6s fit in 5?
  • Bring down every digit: his answer must use every digit of the dividend. 552 ÷ 6 = 9 R1 means he stopped early. The check catches it: 6 × 9 + 1 = 55, not 552.
  • Remainder size: the remainder must be smaller than the divisor. 12 R11 for 95 ÷ 7, or 28 R24 for 696 ÷ 24, pass the multiplication check, so he also needs this rule.
  • Multiples list for 2-digit divisors: he writes it on the side BEFORE dividing, by adding the divisor each time, and checks the 5th multiple with multiplication. Guessing without the list leads to erasing.
  • The check: divisor × quotient + remainder = dividend, written under every problem. This is his Lesson 1 multiplication doing a job; watch his regrouping in the multiplication too.
  • Typing remainders: the answer format is 92 R5 (spaces and lower case are fine). Watch for 92.5 or 925. He has lost points on typed answers that were right on paper, so he checks the typed answer against his paper before pressing Check.
  • Remainder in a story (after-class lesson, not taught in class): he rereads the question after dividing. Round up when everything needs a place (vans, buses, boxes for ALL), drop it when only full groups count, and give the remainder when the question asks what is left. Typing 7 R2 for a number of boxes is the mistake to catch.
  • Reading: he reads fast and accurately, so push for exact words from the text as proof. 'Because I think so' is not evidence. Compare uses both/also; contrast uses but/while/instead.
