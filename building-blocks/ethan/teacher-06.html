Teacher Plan · Lesson 6: The Dairy Farm Schedule and The Night the Calf Came Early

Wednesday, December 16, 2026 · In person · 60 minutes · Week 11: The Dairy Farm Schedule · Focus: Factors, multiples and prime numbers + narrative writing: punctuating dialogue (Pathways Week 11)

🎯 Objective: Math (35 min): Ethan finds every factor pair of a number by testing in order and stopping when the pairs meet, counts factors correctly (a square number like 64 counts its middle factor once), sorts numbers into prime and composite (1 is neither, 2 is the only even prime), uses the divisibility rules for 2, 3, 5, 9 and 10 and tests 7 before calling a number prime, finds common multiples and the least common multiple by skip-counting the bigger number, finds the greatest common factor from two factor lists, and decides whether a word problem needs the LCM (“together again”) or the GCF (“identical groups, nothing left over”), including the second step (how many in each group). Reading (25 min): he punctuates dialogue with the tag first and the tag last (comma placement, capital on the first spoken word, question marks and exclamation points inside), starts a new paragraph for each new speaker, uses strong tags sparingly, and explains how dialogue shows character and moves the plot in a short narrative.
Student goal: I can find factor pairs, tell prime from composite numbers, use the LCM and the GCF to plan a schedule and pack crates, and punctuate dialogue correctly.
Mastery to unlock the next lesson: 85

Watch for

  • Stopping too early or too late on factor pairs: he must test every number in order up to where the pairs meet (6 × 7 for 42). Missing a middle pair is the most common slip (for example leaving out 9 for 63, or 4 for 52). A column of pairs on paper fixes it.
  • Counting factors of square numbers: 64 has 7 factors, not 8, because 8 × 8 counts 8 once. Same for 49, 81 and 100.
  • Odd means prime: 27, 87, 91 and 119 all look prime. He must test 3 (digit sum) AND 7 before deciding. 1 is neither prime nor composite; 2 is prime even though it is even.
  • LCM = multiply the numbers: it is only true when the numbers share no factor (4 and 9). For 8 and 12 the LCM is 24, not 96; for 10 and 15 it is 30, not 150. Adding the numbers (8 + 12 = 20) is a sign he is guessing.
  • Stopping at a common factor instead of the GREATEST: 4 for 16 and 40 (it is 8). And on two-step crate questions, he must read whether it asks for the number of crates (the GCF) or how many in each crate (divide by the GCF).
  • Factor vs multiple: factors divide the number (they are the same size or smaller); multiples are the number times 1, 2, 3 … (the same size or bigger). Pathways asks him to TYPE factors, multiples and divisible.
  • Adding partial products (from Lesson 1): the warm-up and recall stack 2-digit numbers. Watch the regrouping when he adds the two rows.
  • Dialogue punctuation: comma outside the quotation mark (“Hold the towel”, she said), a period instead of a comma before a tag, a capital on the tag pronoun (He said), a missing capital on the first spoken word, and the question mark outside. The rule: everything that belongs to the speaker goes inside.
  • Pathways Week 11 typed answers: both the math and reading pages have Fix the Sentence questions that need the whole sentence retyped EXACTLY (same words, every capital, comma, quotation mark and end mark; straight or curly quotes both work). They also check its/it’s, commas in a list, run-ons and verbs that match their subjects. The reading “Using the Words” questions accept an answer that contains the key word (tag, speaker, narrative). The math number answers must be exact. Pathways gives 2 tries per question (our lessons give 3).
