My Story

Why I'm doing this

I've been part of Habitat for Humanity since high school. Years later I moved into Global Village, Habitat's international build program, and it's taken me to Romania, to Ethiopia — where I built alongside President Jimmy Carter — and to India.

This November, for the first time, I'm leading my own team.

I'm doing this alongside my husband, Fabrice — our first Global Village trip together as a married couple — and a team of volunteers who said yes before they even knew exactly what they were saying yes to. That's the kind of trust I don't take lightly, and it's why I wanted a single place to lay out the whole story clearly: who we're helping, what the week looks like, and how you can be part of it, whether that means picking up a trowel or simply helping fund the work.

The Mission

This isn't a house build. It's a water build.

We're headed to Caruaru, in the sertão — the semi-arid interior of Pernambuco, in Brazil's northeast. We're building cisterns: concrete rainwater tanks that give a village dependable access to clean water. In the sertão, rain arrives hard and briefly, then disappears for months. Without a place to store it, families spend their days walking for whatever water they can find — a walk that usually falls to the women and children, in hours they'd otherwise be in school.

A cistern ends that walk. One tank, properly built, means a family draws water at home through the entire dry season.

We work seven days beside the families themselves and local masons, eat lunch in a neighbor's home each day, and learn a little Portuguese — badly and enthusiastically. On Thursday, November 26 — Thanksgiving — we'll be on the build site. That's the honest catch of this trip, and also, I think, the reason to go: on the one day we set aside to be grateful for what fills our table, we'll be mixing concrete for a tank that holds a family's drinking water through the next dry season.

Before You Go

Visa requirement — U.S. & Canadian citizens

Brazil currently requires U.S. and Canadian citizens to obtain an e-visa before travel. It's handled entirely online through the one official portal, run by VFS Global on behalf of the Brazilian government: brazil.vfsevisa.com. The fee is about $80.90, processing typically takes around 5 business days, and once approved it's valid for multiple entries over several years. I'd strongly encourage applying at least a month or two before departure rather than waiting.
