🧠 Grammar Focus
Talking About Ability 💪

CAN · COULD · WILL BE ABLE TO

Parler de ce que tu sais faire : maintenant, avant, et dans le futur

💡 Key Idea
In English, we use special words called modal verbs to talk about ability — the power to do something. The word we choose depends on whether we're talking about the present, past, or future.
💡 Idée clé
En anglais, on utilise des verbes modaux pour parler de capacité — le pouvoir de faire quelque chose. Le mot qu'on choisit dépend du moment : présent, passé ou futur.
⚙️ The 3 Ability Forms · Les 3 Formes de Capacité
Present
Présent
CAN + base verb
🏊‍♀️ "I can swim 1,500 meters without stopping."
→ Je peux nager 1 500 mètres sans m'arrêter.
⚠️ Negative: "I cannot / can't do butterfly stroke yet."
→ Je ne peux pas encore faire la nage papillon.
Past
Passé
COULD + base verb
🏊‍♀️ "Last year, I could not do a flip turn — now I can!"
→ L'année dernière, je n'arrivais pas à faire un virage-culbute.
⚠️ Negative: "I couldn't qualify when I was 12."
→ Je ne pouvais pas me qualifier quand j'avais 12 ans.
Future
Futur
WILL BE ABLE TO + base verb
🏊‍♀️ "Next year, I will be able to compete at the nationals!"
→ L'année prochaine, je pourrai participer aux championnats nationaux!
🚫 NEVER say: "I will can swim" — This does NOT exist in English!
→ "Will can" n'existe PAS en anglais! Toujours utiliser WILL BE ABLE TO.

The Making of a Champion

Zoé is a 14-year-old swimmer from a small town in France. She trains six days a week — early mornings before school and longer sessions on weekends. It is not always easy. When she was younger, she could only swim the breaststroke, and she could not finish a 400-meter race without stopping to rest.

But Zoé is determined. Every week, she pushes herself to improve her technique a little more. Her coach says her performance has gotten better every single month. This year, Zoé can compete in four different strokes — freestyle, breaststroke, backstroke, and butterfly.

Zoé's biggest goal is to qualify for the national championships before her 16th birthday. She believes that if she keeps training with the same dedication, she will be able to achieve her dream. "Right now, I can push myself harder than ever," she says. "And next season, I will be able to break the regional record."

Zoé's family — her parents, her sister, and even her cat Biscuit — come to every competition to cheer for her. Their support means everything to her.
