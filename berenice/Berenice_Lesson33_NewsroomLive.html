Mission · Mission

📡 Mission Briefing

Today, you're stepping into the Newsroom. Reporters and storytellers don't just quote — they report what people said. To do that, English shifts the tense ONE step into the past. Master this and you'll sound mature and natural.
Aujourd'hui, direction la Newsroom. Les reporters ne citent pas — ils rapportent ce que les gens ont dit. Pour ça, l'anglais recule le temps d'UN cran. Maîtrise-le et tu sonneras mature et naturelle.

🎯 Three goals · Trois objectifs

1. Convert direct → reported speech (statements)
2. Report questions and commands correctly
3. Handle time + place + person changes
1. Convertir direct → indirect (affirmations)
2. Rapporter questions et ordres
3. Adapter le temps, le lieu, les personnes
💡 French does this too: "Je suis fatiguée" → "Elle a dit qu'elle était fatiguée" (NOT "est"). The same backshift! French uses imparfait where English uses Past Simple. You already know this rule.
💡 Le français fait pareil : "Je suis fatiguée" → "Elle a dit qu'elle était fatiguée". Même décalage ! Le français passe à l'imparfait, l'anglais au Past Simple. Tu connais déjà la règle !

🎙️ The 8 Reporter's Rules

Tap each card to flip it and reveal the transformation rule.
Touche chaque carte pour découvrir la règle de transformation.
🗣️
SAY vs TELL
tap to reveal · tape pour découvrir
SAY vs TELL
SAY (something) — no person needed.
TELL (someone something) — person required.
SAY = dire (sans complément). TELL = dire à (avec personne).
"He said he was tired." / "He told me he was tired."
⏪
Present → Past
tap to reveal · tape pour découvrir
Present → Past
Present Simple becomes Past Simple in reported speech
Le présent devient passé simple
"I am happy" → She said she WAS happy.
⏮️
Past → Past Perfect
tap to reveal · tape pour découvrir
Past → Past Perfect
Past Simple shifts to Past Perfect
Le passé simple devient plus-que-parfait
"I went home" → He said he HAD GONE home.
🔮
Will → Would
tap to reveal · tape pour découvrir
Will → Would
Future "will" becomes "would" in reported speech
"Will" devient "would" en discours indirect
"I will help" → She said she WOULD help.
💪
Can → Could / Must → Had to
tap to reveal · tape pour découvrir
Modal Backshift
Modals shift back: can → could, may → might, must → had to
Les modaux reculent aussi
"I can swim" → He said he COULD swim.
📅
Time + Place Words
tap to reveal · tape pour découvrir
Time + Place Shift
today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, here → there, this → that
today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, here → there
"I'll see you tomorrow" → She said she'd see me THE NEXT DAY.
❓
Reported Questions
tap to reveal · tape pour découvrir
Reported Questions
Yes/No → use IF/WHETHER. Drop question word order. Use ASKED.
Yes/No → IF/WHETHER. Pas d'inversion. ASKED.
"Are you OK?" → He asked IF I WAS OK.
📣
Reported Commands
tap to reveal · tape pour découvrir
Reported Commands
Use TOLD/ASKED + person + TO + verb. Negative: NOT TO.
TOLD/ASKED + personne + TO + verbe. Négation : NOT TO.
"Sit down!" → He told me TO SIT DOWN.

📊 The Big Backshift Rule

When you report what someone said, the verb steps ONE tense backwards. Watch it happen:
Quand tu rapportes ce qu'on a dit, le verbe recule D'UN CRAN. Regarde :

was / were

📰 Direct Speech

The exact words inside quotes:
I am tired.
→ Berenice's exact words

🎙️ Reported Speech

When YOU tell someone else:
Berenice said she was tired.
→ "am" → "was" (backshift!)

🔑 Reporter's Three Checklist

When converting direct → reported, ALWAYS check:
1. The VERB tense (one step back)
2. The PRONOUNS ("I" → "she/he", "my" → "her/his")
3. The TIME / PLACE words (today → that day, here → there)
Quand tu convertis direct → indirect, vérifie TOUJOURS :
1. Le TEMPS du verbe (un cran en arrière)
2. Les PRONOMS ("I" → "she/he")
3. Les mots de TEMPS / LIEU
🇫🇷 French parallel: "Je viendrai demain" → "Il a dit qu'il viendrait le lendemain". French shifts futur → conditionnel AND demain → le lendemain. Same logic in English with would + the next day.
🇫🇷 Parallèle français : "Je viendrai demain" → "Il a dit qu'il viendrait le lendemain". Le français déplace le verbe ET les indices de temps. Identique en anglais !
