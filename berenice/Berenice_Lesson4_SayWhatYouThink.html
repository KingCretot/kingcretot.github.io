Part 2 · When someone disagrees

🤝 Answering Someone Who Disagrees

The secret: first show you listened, THEN give your view. People listen to you when you listen to them.
Le secret : montre d'abord que tu as écouté, PUIS donne ton avis. On t'écoute quand tu écoutes les autres.
✅Show you listened"I see your point, but…" / "That's fair, but…"
Je comprends ton point de vue, mais… / C'est juste, mais…
✅Agree with one part"You're right that…, but I still think…"
Tu as raison sur…, mais je pense quand même que…
✅Disagree softly"I'm not sure I agree, because…"
Je ne suis pas sûre d'être d'accord, parce que…
❌Don't say — too rude"You're wrong." / "That's stupid."
À éviter : ça ferme la conversation.

Meet the Guest · Rencontre avec l'invitée

📋 Meet Today's Guest

Guest Bio · Fiche invitée

Meet today's guest: Sofia Marchetti, 15, from Milan. This year, Sofia's school made a new rule: phones must stay in lockers all day. Sofia thinks the rule goes too far.

In her opinion, phones can be useful in class. Her English teacher sometimes asks students to look up a word or check a map. She also feels like teenagers should learn to manage their phones themselves, because they will have to do it as adults anyway.

Her grandmother, Nonna Lucia, completely disagrees. "When I was young, we talked to each other at lunch," she says. "Phones steal your attention."

Sofia used to get angry in these conversations. Now she tries something different. "I see your point, Nonna. Phones can be distracting — that's fair. But I still think a total ban goes too far. Maybe phones could be allowed when the teacher says so."

Nonna laughed. "Okay. That's more reasonable than I expected." They still don't agree — but for the first time, they both really listened.

Sofia, 15 ans, de Milan. Son école a interdit les téléphones toute la journée. Sofia pense que la règle va trop loin : les téléphones peuvent être utiles en classe, et les ados doivent apprendre à gérer leur téléphone eux-mêmes. Sa grand-mère n'est pas du tout d'accord : les téléphones volent l'attention. Avant, Sofia s'énervait. Maintenant, elle reconnaît d'abord que sa grand-mère a un point juste, puis elle donne son avis et propose une solution. Elles ne sont toujours pas d'accord, mais elles se sont vraiment écoutées.
