The Case File · Le dossier

🗝️ Mission Briefing

Welcome to the Mansion, Detective Berenice. Today's case: relative clauses — the connecting words that turn two short sentences into ONE smooth, descriptive one. Master them and your English will sound elegant and adult.
Bienvenue au Manoir, Détective Bérénice. Aujourd'hui : les propositions relatives — les mots de liaison qui transforment deux phrases courtes en UNE phrase fluide et descriptive. Maîtrise-les et ton anglais sonnera élégant et adulte.

🎯 Three goals · Trois objectifs

1. Pick the right relative pronoun (who / which / that / where / whose / when)
2. Combine 2 sentences using a relative clause
3. Tell defining vs non-defining apart (commas matter!)
1. Choisir le bon pronom relatif
2. Combiner 2 phrases en une
3. Distinguer les défis avec/sans virgules
💡 You already do this in French: "C'est la fille qui habite à côté." / "Le livre que j'ai lu." / "La ville où je suis née." French has qui / que / où / dont — English has who / which / that / where / whose. One-to-one map.
💡 Tu fais déjà ça en français : qui / que / où / dont. En anglais : who / which / that / where / whose. C'est une correspondance directe.

For places

For possession ('s)

🚪 Defining (no commas)

ESSENTIAL info — tells you WHICH one we mean:
"The book that Berenice gave me is on the desk."
→ Which book? The one Berenice gave me. Without it, we don't know.

📜 Non-defining (with commas)

EXTRA info — bonus detail you could remove:
"Berenice, who swims six days a week, is fit."
→ Extra info between commas. Sentence still works without it.

⚠️ The Detective's Two Rules

RULE 1: If you can REMOVE the clause and the sentence still makes sense → it's non-defining → use COMMAS.

RULE 2: NEVER use THAT in non-defining clauses (with commas). Only WHO, WHICH, WHERE, WHEN, WHOSE.
RÈGLE 1 : Si tu peux ENLEVER la proposition et que la phrase tient debout → c'est non-définie → VIRGULES.

RÈGLE 2 : JAMAIS THAT dans les non-définies (avec virgules). Seulement WHO, WHICH, WHERE, WHEN, WHOSE.
