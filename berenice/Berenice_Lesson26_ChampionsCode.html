🧠 Grammar Focus
SHOULD · MUST · HAVE TO 📋

Three ways to express obligation, advice and rules

Trois façons d'exprimer l'obligation, les conseils et les règles

💡 Key Idea
You already know CAN, COULD, and WILL BE ABLE TO for ability. Today we learn three new modal forms for obligation. The word we choose tells us how strong the rule is and where it comes from.
💡 Idée clé
Tu connais déjà CAN, COULD et WILL BE ABLE TO pour la capacité. Aujourd'hui on apprend trois nouvelles formes modales pour l'obligation. Le mot choisi indique à quel point la règle est forte et d'où elle vient.
⚡ Obligation Strength Scale · L'échelle d'obligation
SHOULD Advice · Conseil C'est une bonne idée
→
MUST Strong · Personnel Obligation interne forte
→
HAVE TO Rule · Règle externe Règle qui vient de dehors
gentle advice / conseil douxexternal rule / règle externe
⚙️ The 3 Obligation Forms · Les 3 Formes d'Obligation
SHOULD
Conseil / Recommandation
SHOULD + base verb
🏊‍♀️ "You should sleep nine hours — it helps you recover."
→ Tu devrais dormir neuf heures — ça aide à récupérer. (conseil, pas obligatoire)
⚠️ Negative: "You shouldn't eat junk food before a competition."
→ Tu ne devrais pas manger de malbouffe avant une compétition.
MUST
Obligation forte / interne
MUST + base verb
🏊‍♀️ "I must focus during every race — it comes from inside me."
→ Je dois absolument me concentrer pendant chaque course. (obligation personnelle forte)
🚨 MUST NOT = FORBIDDEN: "You must not skip your warm-up — it causes injuries."
→ MUSTN'T = absolument interdit. Attention : c'est différent de DON'T HAVE TO!
HAVE TO
Règle externe / nécessité
HAVE TO / HAS TO + base verb
🏊‍♀️ "She has to arrive 20 minutes early — it is the club rule."
→ Elle doit arriver 20 minutes avant — c'est la règle du club. (vient de l'extérieur)
⚠️ DON'T HAVE TO = NOT NECESSARY: "She doesn't have to wear a club cap on weekends."
→ DON'T HAVE TO ≠ MUSTN'T! "Pas obligatoire" ≠ "interdit"!
🚨 Critical Difference · Différence Critique
❌ "You must not eat that." = It is FORBIDDEN.
"Tu ne dois absolument pas manger ça." = C'est INTERDIT.
✅ "You don't have to eat that." = It is NOT NECESSARY (but allowed).
"Tu n'es pas obligé(e) de manger ça." = Pas OBLIGATOIRE (mais c'est permis).

Zoé's Champion Code

Being a great swimmer means more than just practicing — it means following a Champion's Code. Zoé's coach, Madame Laurent, gave her three key rules to live by.

💛
Rule 1 — MUST: "You must sleep at least nine hours every night." Sleep is when muscles recover and the mind recharges. Without enough rest, Zoé cannot perform at her best. Must comes from inside — it is Zoé's personal commitment to her body.
💚
Rule 2 — SHOULD: "You should eat pasta and vegetables before every competition." This is not a strict rule — it is advice from Coach Laurent. Zoé should also drink two liters of water on race day. It is not required, but it is strongly recommended.
❤️
Rule 3 — HAVE TO: "You have to arrive at the pool twenty minutes early." This is a club rule — not her personal choice. Every swimmer in the team has to follow it. Zoé has to warm up, check her equipment, and listen to her coach's strategy for the race.

Zoé takes her Champion's Code seriously. She is consistent and disciplined. "I must stay focused," she says. "I know that if I follow the code, I will be able to achieve my goals and make my family proud."
🔎 Focus Challenge
Can you spot the difference? In the text, Rule 1 uses MUST (personal commitment), Rule 2 uses SHOULD (advice), Rule 3 uses HAVE TO (external club rule). Tell your tutor: why is each word used? Think about where the obligation comes from! 🧠
🔎 Défi de concentration
Repères-tu la différence? Dans le texte, Règle 1 = MUST (engagement personnel), Règle 2 = SHOULD (conseil), Règle 3 = HAVE TO (règle externe du club). Dis à ton tuteur : pourquoi chaque mot est-il utilisé? Pense à l'origine de l'obligation!
