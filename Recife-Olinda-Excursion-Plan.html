The shape of the trip

Everyone flies into Recife (REC) on Wednesday, November 18. A private driver Jonathan is arranging will run airport pickups to our pousada in Olinda (20–30 min) as flights land — send him your flight details when you book. No group program that night; arrive when you arrive. Then we get three full days together.

Thursday — Olinda on foot

A guided walking day through the colonial hill town we're sleeping in: working artist studios, baroque churches, the old market in a former slave-trade building, the giant carnival puppets — capped with sunset over Recife and a welcome dinner at one of the best restaurants in the Northeast.

Friday — Black Consciousness Day

November 20 is a national holiday, and Pernambuco is the heartland of Afro-Brazilian maracatu culture. We visit the surreal Brennand ceramic sculpture compound by day and catch free holiday drum performances in the evening — something no tour package sells.

Saturday — Recife

The big-city day: a 19th-century iron market, the interactive frevo dance museum, the Cais do Sertão museum (the story of the dry interior region we'll be building in), lunch with live music at a market beer garden, and a communal farewell feast back in Olinda.

Sunday — the Habitat itinerary takes over

We check out of the pousada Sunday morning and our private driver takes the group to our Habitat-provided lodging in Recife, where we join the arriving team. From there it's the official program: lunch at a local restaurant, the 2-hour drive to Caruaru, hotel check-in, orientation, and the welcome dinner.

Who this is for: everyone — all paces welcome. Olinda's historic center is steep cobblestone — bring sturdy walking shoes — and every day has an easy opt-out (pool, café, terrace). Nothing is mandatory; this whole excursion is optional and separate from the $2,300 build program cost.

Good to know

  • Money: Brazil uses the real (R$), recently around R$5–5.5 per US dollar. Cards are accepted almost everywhere; carry R$100–200 in small bills for markets, tapioca stalls, and tips. Airport ATMs work with US cards.
  • WhatsApp is essential in Brazil — install it before you fly. The trip group chat, the pousada, guides, and restaurants all run on it.
  • Getting around: our private driver covers group movements; for anything on your own, Uber is cheap, safe, and everywhere (a cross-town ride is usually $3–8). Nobody needs to rent a car.
  • Weather in November: start of the dry season — sunny, mid-80s to low 90s °F with ocean breeze. Pack light clothing, a hat, strong sunscreen, and a refillable water bottle.
  • Footwear: Olinda is steep cobblestone. Sturdy walking shoes or grippy sneakers, not flip-flops, for the walking days.
  • Safety basics: Olinda's historic center and Recife Antigo are tourist-friendly by day; use normal big-city sense — minimal jewelry, phone in front pocket, Ubers after dark, stick together in the São José market area.
  • Health: check the CDC's current Brazil guidance with your doctor (routine vaccines; many travelers also get Hep A/typhoid). Drink bottled or filtered water. Pharmacies ("farmácias") are abundant and well-stocked.
  • Language: Portuguese; English is limited outside hotels. Google Translate's camera mode + a smile covers the rest.
  • Phone: check your carrier's international plan, or grab an eSIM (Airalo etc.) before you fly.
